The question is which books repay a monthly Audible credit, and which are an episode stretched to book length.

This is the short list: eight titles we would spend a credit on, five more for a spare one, each paired with shows from our catalogue of 700+ podcasts.

## How to use a credit if you already binge podcasts

An Audible membership gives you a monthly credit. One credit buys one title, whatever its length or list price, and titles you buy with credits stay in your library. Check Audible's current terms for the detail on unused credits.

Three rules:

- **Spend credits on the long books.** A credit doesn't care whether the book runs six hours or twenty.
- **Start with a case you already know.** Pick the book nearest to a podcast you have finished. Familiarity carries you through the first hour.
- **Treat chapters like episodes.** Listen in the slots you already use.

## The eight titles worth a credit

### I'll Be Gone in the Dark — Michelle McNamara

McNamara spent years on the man she named the Golden State Killer, responsible for a series of rapes and murders across California from the mid-1970s to the mid-1980s. She died in April 2016 with the book unfinished. Researcher Paul Haynes and journalist Billy Jensen completed it, with her husband, Patton Oswalt, seeing it through, and it was published in February 2018. Two months later police arrested Joseph James DeAngelo, identified through genetic genealogy. He pleaded guilty in 2020 to thirteen murders.

**In audio.** Gabra Zackman reads it. McNamara's voice, part case file and part confession, is the thing to hold on to, and one reader keeps it steady through the reconstructed sections.

**Listen next if you finished** Crime Junkie's run of cold cases and want the single-case version: one person, years of paperwork, an ending that arrived from outside. More in [cold case podcasts](/category/cold-cases) and [podcasts like Crime Junkie](/podcasts-like/crime-junkie).

**Skip it if** you need the person who hunted him to write the ending. McNamara never saw the arrest. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=I%27ll+Be+Gone+in+the+Dark+Michelle+McNamara+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### Say Nothing — Patrick Radden Keefe

In December 1972 Jean McConville, a widow and mother of ten, was taken from her flat in Belfast in front of her children. The IRA admitted in 1999 that it had killed her. Her remains were found on a County Louth beach in 2003. Keefe uses her disappearance to tell the Troubles through people rather than factions: Dolours Price and Brendan Hughes, who were in the IRA, and the legal fight over the interviews they gave to a Boston College oral history project. It is the best-reported book here.

**In audio.** A large cast across fifty years, told in sections short enough to pick up after a week away. That is podcast structure with a book's depth.

**Listen next if you finished** the first season of Serial or In the Dark and want reporting that is as patient and less certain of itself. Start with [podcasts like Serial](/podcasts-like/serial), then [investigative podcasts](/category/investigative), or [UK-made shows](/country/UK) for the British end.

**Skip it if** you want a solved case by chapter twelve. Some questions stay open, and the book is clear about which. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Say+Nothing+Patrick+Radden+Keefe+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### The Five — Hallie Rubenhold

Mary Ann Nichols, Annie Chapman, Elizabeth Stride, Catherine Eddowes and Mary Jane Kelly are known as Jack the Ripper's victims, and for most of a century and a half that is all they have been. Rubenhold gives each a biography: childhood, marriage, children, work, and the bad luck that left them in Whitechapel in 1888. She argues that the assumption most of them sold sex rests on thin evidence. Some historians dispute that. The killer is absent on purpose.

**In audio.** Five self-contained lives in sequence. With no cliff-hanger to rush towards, you take it one woman at a time.

**Listen next if you finished** a historical episode of UK True Crime Podcast and wanted more of the people in it. Our [best British true crime podcasts](/blog/best-british-true-crime-podcasts) guide covers the rest of the country's record.

**Skip it if** you came for forensics and suspect theories. The Ripper is barely in it. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=The+Five+Hallie+Rubenhold+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### In Cold Blood — Truman Capote

In November 1959 Herbert and Bonnie Clutter and two of their children, Nancy and Kenyon, were killed at their farmhouse in Holcomb, Kansas. Capote spent years on the case and published it in 1966 as a "nonfiction novel". Perry Smith and Richard Hickock were hanged in 1965. It is the ancestor of modern true crime, and it is flawed. Capote reconstructed scenes and invented connective detail while presenting the book as immaculately factual, and his closeness to Smith shapes where its sympathy falls. Read it as a foundation, not a record.

**In audio.** The structure, alternating the Clutters' last day with the killers' road trip, has forward motion when read aloud. We can't vouch for any one narrator, so take the standard Audible edition.

**Listen next if you finished** Serial and started wondering how far to trust a storyteller. [Podcasts like Serial](/podcasts-like/serial) are its heirs. [Podcasts like Casefile](/podcasts-like/casefile-true-crime) take the opposite method: sourced and deliberately flat. [How we review](/how-we-review) sets out how we weigh sourcing.

**Skip it if** you need modern sourcing on every page. Capote gives you no notes to check. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=In+Cold+Blood+Truman+Capote+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### Empire of Pain — Patrick Radden Keefe

The Sackler family built OxyContin into a blockbuster through Purdue Pharma while the overdoses climbed. Keefe follows three generations: the brothers Arthur, Mortimer and Raymond, the fortune and the lawsuits. Purdue has pleaded guilty twice, over misleading marketing in 2007 and to federal charges in 2020, and went through bankruptcy. The family itself has settled civil claims rather than stood trial. Behind the ledger are the people who became addicted, and the families who buried them.

**In audio.** A corporate saga with a big cast, made easy to follow by Keefe's scene-setting. It is long, which makes it a good use of a credit.

**Listen next if you finished** The Shrink Next Door or The Dropout and want the same question at a larger scale: what happens when a reputation outlasts the evidence. Our [fraud and scam podcasts](/category/fraud-scams) go further.

**Skip it if** you want a prison-cell ending. It ends in settlements and bankruptcy court. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Empire+of+Pain+Patrick+Radden+Keefe+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### Mindhunter — John E. Douglas and Mark Olshaker

Douglas was an FBI agent who, from the 1970s, interviewed imprisoned serial killers to understand how they offended. The work helped shape the Bureau's Behavioral Science Unit and what we now call profiling. Published in 1995, the book is his memoir of it, written with Mark Olshaker, and the source for the Netflix series. It is drier than the series. Profiling has since faced sustained criticism over its reliability, so read it as a first-hand record of how the FBI came to think, not settled science. It is written from the offender's side of the table; the victims appear as case files.

**In audio.** Interview-led, episodic chapters. It plays like a long conversation, and you can stop and start without losing the thread.

**Listen next if you finished** the Netflix series, or a run of serial-offender cases on Casefile, and want the source. [Best true crime podcasts](/best-true-crime-podcasts) for what follows.

**Skip it if** you have had enough of the serial-killer canon. Go back to *The Five*, which tells that history from the other side. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Mindhunter+John+Douglas+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### If You Tell — Gregg Olsen

Gregg Olsen tells the story of the Christensen sisters, who grew up in rural Washington State in a household where their mother abused the children and other people living there. Two deaths came to light only years later, once the sisters were adults. The book stays close to their account. It is not the most important title here. It is a big seller on Audible, and the one weekly listeners tend to finish.

**In audio.** The most podcast-shaped book here: a domestic setting, testimony-led, short chapters that behave like episodes of a season.

**Listen next if you finished** Crime Junkie and want the same pace at season length. Our [binge-worthy podcasts](/category/binge-worthy) are the audio equivalent, and [podcasts like Crime Junkie](/podcasts-like/crime-junkie) cover the weekly habit.

**Skip it if** you want independent reporting. It leans on the sisters' account, which is powerful but one side of a house. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=If+You+Tell+Gregg+Olsen+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

### Unnatural Causes — Dr Richard Shepherd

Shepherd was a British forensic pathologist whose career took in the Hillsborough disaster and decades of other casework. His 2018 memoir is not one case. It is a career, told from the examination table rather than the crime scene, and it is careful about what a post-mortem can and cannot establish. The dead are people to him, not evidence. For a listener worn out by American cases, it is a British change of scene.

**In audio.** First person, conversational, organised case by case. It sounds like a long interview with someone who has earned the right to be unhurried.

**Listen next if you finished** Morbid and want a forensic expert telling it straight, or UK True Crime Podcast and want the view from the mortuary. More in [British true crime podcasts](/country/UK).

**Skip it if** you need a single narrative hook in the first ten minutes. It builds by accumulation. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Unnatural+Causes+Richard+Shepherd+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

## If you only take one credit this month

- **The best book:** *Say Nothing*. Nothing else here is as well reported.
- **The book that grew out of podcast culture:** *I'll Be Gone in the Dark*. An amateur's obsessive investigation, taken up by the genre's audience. It is the same impulse as a listener giving up a Saturday to a case.
- **For the British listener tired of American cases:** *Unnatural Causes*.
- **The credit you will actually use:** *If You Tell*. It is the one most likely to be finished.

## Also worth a credit

Five more, if you have a spare credit. Each is a factual description, not a review.

- *The Devil in the White City* (Erik Larson): Chicago's 1893 World's Fair, with the murders of H. H. Holmes as the smaller thread. The fair is the main character, and the Holmes legend has been questioned since. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Devil+in+the+White+City+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)
- *Killers of the Flower Moon* (David Grann): the murders of Osage people in 1920s Oklahoma for their oil money, and the early FBI's part in solving them. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Killers+of+the+Flower+Moon+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)
- *The Wager* (David Grann): the 1741 wreck of HMS Wager and the rival accounts of mutiny that followed. True crime adjacent: testimony and competing stories rather than a murder case. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=The+Wager+David+Grann+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)
- *Chaos* (Tom O'Neill): a long challenge to the official account of the Manson murders. Read it as an argument, not a settled account. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=Chaos+Tom+O%27Neill+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)
- *The Stranger Beside Me* (Ann Rule): Rule knew Ted Bundy from a Seattle crisis line before she wrote about him, and says how slowly she accepted what the evidence showed. [Listen on Audible via Amazon (affiliate)](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=The+Stranger+Beside+Me+Ann+Rule+Audible&tag=PLACEHOLDER)

## Audiobooks are not the same as podcasts on Audible

Audible sells audiobooks. It also carries podcasts and Audible Originals, which is where the confusion starts.

Two pages, two jobs. Our [Audible platform page](/platform/Audible) lists the **shows** that live there: West Cork, Blood Will Tell, British Scandal, The Shrink Next Door and The Apology Line. This page covers **books**. A podcast is reported and released in episodes. An audiobook is a finished book read aloud. Whether a given show is included with membership or free to anyone differs by show and by country, so check before you rely on it.

## Is Audible worth it if you already pay for Spotify?

Audible vs Spotify for true crime is not a contest, because they hold different things. Spotify is where the weekly shows live, and our guide to [true crime podcasts on Spotify](/blog/true-crime-podcasts-spotify) covers what is there. Some Spotify Premium plans include a monthly audiobook allowance from a limited catalogue. Check the title you want is in it.

Audible's case is the credit: one title of any length, kept in your library. That suits the long books on this list.

Be honest about how you listen. If you will finish a book every month or two, Audible earns its place next to Spotify. If you will listen to one this year, borrow it from your library app first.
