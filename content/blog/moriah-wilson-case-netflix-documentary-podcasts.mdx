**Moriah "Mo" Wilson was a 25-year-old gravel and mountain bike racer, and the brightest young talent in American off-road cycling, when she was shot dead in Austin, Texas, on 11 May 2022.** Kaitlin Armstrong, the girlfriend of cyclist Colin Strickland, fled to Costa Rica on her sister's passport and was caught after 43 days. In November 2023 a jury convicted her of first-degree murder and sentenced her to 90 years in prison. Netflix's documentary *The Truth and Tragedy of Moriah Wilson* was released on 3 April 2026.

The documentary's makers say they wanted to put Mo back at the centre of a story that headlines had turned into a "love triangle". This guide tries to do the same. It starts with who she was and what she achieved, then explains what happened in Austin, the investigation, the trial and where the case stands now, before reviewing the Netflix film and recommending the podcasts worth your time.

Facts here come from the [Texas Third Court of Appeals' 2026 opinion](https://search.txcourts.gov/Case.aspx?cn=03-23-00792-CR&coa=coa03), which summarises the trial evidence, and from reporting by [Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/netflix-documentary-about-murdered-cyclist-mo-wilson-premiers-at-sxsw), [VeloNews](https://sports.yahoo.com/mo-wilson-blasts-field-win-230228682.html), [FOX 7 Austin](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/kaitlin-armstrong-texas-appeals-court-affirms-murder-conviction), [CBS Austin](https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/texas-appeals-court-denies-new-trial-for-kaitlin-armstrong-in-mo-wilson-murder-case), the [Austin American-Statesman](https://www.statesman.com/news/article/moriah-wilson-murder-documentary-netflix-austin-22091807.php) and [The Dartmouth](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2026/04/wissman-moriah-documentary), plus Netflix's own material, all checked on 25 November 2026. ## Who was Moriah Wilson?

Anna Moriah Wilson was born in New Hampshire in May 1996 and grew up in Kirby, in the Northeast Kingdom of Vermont, with her parents Karen and Eric and her younger brother Matt. It was an outdoor family: her father had been an alpine ski racer and coach, she told [The Gravel Ride](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/moriah-wilson-2021-breakout-gravel-racing-season/id1352602663?i=1000545631868) in 2021, and she spent her summers mountain biking with her parents and friends. She went to [Burke Mountain Academy](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2026/04/wissman-moriah-documentary), the ski racing school near her home. A school friend remembers her as "the strongest, fastest biker even in high school. She would beat all the boys."

Getting into Dartmouth College had been her dream since she was about 11, her brother told *The Dartmouth*. She arrived in 2015 after a gap year, raced on the alpine ski team and studied engineering, graduating in 2019. John Dwyer, who coached her at both Burke and Dartmouth, said her work ethic "separated her from everyone else on the team. She just grinded hard and never complained about anything… She was pretty much the perfect teammate."

After graduating she moved to Marin County, California, to work for the bike maker Specialized, and started racing bikes locally. Friends describe someone who was intensely driven but not comfortable at the centre of attention. "I don't think it came naturally for her to be at the center of attention in any way," Matt Wilson [told VeloNews](https://velo.outsideonline.com/gravel/the-moriah-wilson-foundation-using-momentum-and-more-to-honor-the-late-cyclists-legacy/). Her plan was to use her growing profile for "community, cycling, sports, youth, and giving back".

## Moriah Wilson's cycling career

Wilson only took up bike racing seriously during the pandemic, and within two seasons she was the rider everyone else in American gravel was measuring themselves against.

- **2021, the breakout year.** Her results in California's Grasshopper Adventure Series got her noticed. She went on to finish second at the Leadville Trail 100 mountain bike race and, in October, won [Big Sugar Gravel](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/netflix-documentary-about-murdered-cyclist-mo-wilson-premiers-at-sxsw) in Bentonville, Arkansas, one of the biggest races on the calendar.
- **Spring 2022, dominance.** She won the Rock Cobbler, the Shasta Gravel Hugger and several Grasshopper races, then beat a field that included Olympians and former WorldTour riders to win the 80km mountain bike race at the Sea Otter Classic, the opening round of the Life Time Grand Prix. In late April she won the 137-mile [Belgian Waffle Ride California](https://sports.yahoo.com/mo-wilson-blasts-field-win-230228682.html) solo, by about 25 minutes.
- **Going full time.** That spring she left Specialized to race full time. She came to Texas in May 2022 for Gravel Locos, a few days early to see her friend Caitlin Cash.

Her rivals were as admiring as anyone. After Wilson dropped her at the Belgian Waffle Ride, runner-up Flavia Oliveira laughed that "washboards are not Flavia friendly" and that she was "not as savvy as a descender like Mo". Dwyer, her old ski coach, recalled a race she won by more than an hour: "I had no doubt in my mind that she would have been the best female bike racer in the country for sure, if not in the world."

If you want to hear her in her own words, a few cycling podcasts recorded long conversations with her in 2021 and 2022. They are listed in the podcast section below, and they are the best antidote to coverage that only knows her as a victim.

## What happened in Austin in May 2022

Unless another source is given, this account follows the [Third Court of Appeals' summary](https://search.txcourts.gov/Case.aspx?cn=03-23-00792-CR&coa=coa03) of the evidence the jury heard.

Strickland, a well-known gravel racer based in Austin, met Wilson at a race in Idaho in September 2021 and again in Bentonville in October. After the Bentonville trip, he testified, his relationship with Armstrong "ended", and he and Wilson dated briefly that autumn. By early November the romance was over, and in December Strickland and Armstrong got back together. He and Wilson stayed friendly.

When Wilson came to Austin in May 2022, she and Strickland arranged to meet. That afternoon, he testified, he deleted their text thread and saved her number under the name "Christine Wall" so as not "to agitate" Armstrong. They went swimming, had dinner, and at about 8:36pm he dropped her back at Cash's garage apartment on his motorcycle and went home. Afterwards, he admitted, he texted Armstrong a false alibi about where he had been.

At about 9:15pm a neighbour's doorbell camera recorded a high-pitched scream followed by three gunshots. When Caitlin Cash came home, she found Moriah and called 911 at 9:54pm. The medical examiner testified that Wilson was shot three times, in the head and chest, and had a wound to her finger consistent with trying to defend herself. Her bike had been taken from the house and thrown into a nearby bush.

## The investigation

Surveillance video showed a black Jeep Grand Cherokee near Cash's home around the time of the shooting. It matched Armstrong's vehicle, and the Jeep's own GPS logs, read at trial, put it right next to the alley beside the apartment from 8:40pm until 9:17pm, two minutes after the shots, when it drove back to the home she shared with Strickland.

The next day Armstrong was arrested on an old, unrelated warrant and questioned for about an hour. She denied knowing that Strickland and Wilson had been in touch. She was then released, reportedly because of a discrepancy between her date of birth on the warrant and in police records. Over the following days:

- **Two friends came forward.** Nicole Mertz told police, and later the jury, that months earlier Armstrong had said that if Strickland seriously dated someone else, "I would kill her." Another acquaintance, Jacqueline Chasteen, testified that Armstrong had said "in so many words" that she wanted to kill Wilson, adding that she had bought a gun or thought about buying one.
- **Armstrong disappeared.** She sold her Jeep to CarMax on 13 May and flew to New York the next day. A murder warrant was issued on 17 May. By then she was gone.

### The search and arrest

On 18 May 2022, Armstrong flew from Newark to San José, Costa Rica, on her sister Christine's passport. For six weeks she moved around under false names, teaching yoga. On 22 June, as "Allison Paige", she had a nose job, lip enhancements and other cosmetic treatment.

The US Marshals traced her with help from the State Department's Diplomatic Security Service, Homeland Security Investigations and Costa Rican authorities. A sign-in sheet at a yoga studio using the alias she had travelled under was among the most important leads, [VeloNews reported](https://velo.outsideonline.com/news/gravel/details-in-kaitlin-armstrong-case-reveal-fraudulent-use-of-passport-and-a-paper-trail-of-yoga-classes-in-costa-rica/). On 29 June 2022, 43 days after the warrant, she was arrested at a hostel in Santa Teresa, on the Nicoya Peninsula, deported and returned to Texas to face the murder charge. She pleaded not guilty.

## The trial: the evidence, the prosecution and the defence

The trial opened on 1 November 2023 in Austin before Judge Brenda Kennedy. Nineteen days earlier, Armstrong had slipped away from two corrections officers on the way to a medical appointment and was caught after a chase of about half a mile. The jury saw video of that too.

### The prosecution's case

The prosecution, led in court by Assistant District Attorney Rickey Jones, called 42 witnesses over eight days, [FOX 7 reported](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/kaitlin-armstrong-trial-state-rests-case-day-9). Their main evidence was:

- **The Jeep.** GPS logs and surveillance placed Armstrong's vehicle beside Cash's apartment from just before Wilson got home until two minutes after the gunshots.
- **The phone.** Armstrong's phone was switched off at 7:30pm and back on at 9:47pm, after the killing.
- **The gun.** A firearms examiner testified that the bullets were fired from Armstrong's SIG Sauer 9mm pistol, the one Strickland had bought her.
- **The DNA.** A Texas DPS analyst testified that Armstrong could not be excluded as a contributor to DNA found on Wilson's bike, which had been moved and dumped in a bush.
- **The address.** A note on Armstrong's iCloud account contained an address that does not exist, "1704 Maple Ave", which brought up Cash's garage apartment on Google Maps. It was deleted the day after the murder.
- **The jealousy.** Texts and Strickland's testimony described Armstrong's anger about Wilson, alongside the two witnesses who said she had talked about killing her.
- **The flight.** Selling the Jeep, using her sister's passport, the false names and the surgery, and the escape attempt, all presented as consciousness of guilt.

### The defence's case

Armstrong's lawyers, Rick Cofer and Geoffrey Puryear, argued the case was entirely circumstantial: no one saw her shoot Wilson, and she made no confession. "The state's purported forensic science is inaccurate, is unreliable and unscientific," Puryear told the jury in his opening. They argued that:

- **The DNA could have been transferred.** A defence expert testified that Armstrong's DNA could have reached Wilson's bike indirectly, from Strickland's motorcycle or helmet via Wilson herself. A prosecution rebuttal expert said direct contact was "much more likely".
- **Firearms identification is subjective.** A defence materials scientist challenged the method used to match bullets to a gun, and the state's examiner agreed under cross-examination that his conclusions were "subjective".
- **The data proves the car, not the driver.** A police analyst agreed that the GPS data could not show who was driving the Jeep, and a fingerprint examiner called by the defence testified that none of the more than 20 prints lifted at the front of the apartment conclusively matched Armstrong. In closing, Cofer told jurors she had been "caught in a nightmare of circumstantial evidence".

Armstrong did not testify.

## The verdict and sentence

On 16 November 2023, after deliberating for about two hours, the jury found Armstrong guilty of murder. The next day the same jury, which in Texas can decide the punishment, set her sentence at 90 years in prison and a $10,000 fine, [Cycling Weekly reported](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/netflix-documentary-about-murdered-cyclist-mo-wilson-premiers-at-sxsw). Afterwards, Moriah's mother Karen and her friend Caitlin Cash addressed Armstrong in court.

Two weeks later, after consulting Wilson's family and law enforcement, the Travis County District Attorney's Office [dropped the separate escape charge](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/kaitlin-armstrong-escape-charges-dismissed), along with a minor theft charge.

## The appeal and where the case stands now

Armstrong appealed. Her appellate lawyers argued that the trial court should have held a hearing on her motion for a new trial, that the state's DNA expert had overstated what "activity level" DNA analysis can show, that her trial lawyers should have presented more mitigating evidence about her background, and that her police interview should have been excluded.

On 23 January 2026 the [Third Court of Appeals rejected every argument](https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/texas-appeals-court-denies-new-trial-for-kaitlin-armstrong-in-mo-wilson-murder-case) and affirmed the conviction, finding "no reversible error". It noted that a difference of opinion between experts does not make testimony false. According to the [court's docket](https://search.txcourts.gov/Case.aspx?cn=03-23-00792-CR&coa=coa03), it then refused to reconsider the case with the full court on 26 June 2026, and on 29 October 2026 the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals, the state's highest criminal court, refused her petition for discretionary review. Her direct appeal is over. She is serving her sentence in the Texas prison system.

Two civil cases followed the criminal one:

- **Wrongful death.** Eric and Karen Wilson sued Armstrong in May 2024. When she did not respond, a Travis County judge entered a [$15 million default judgment](https://www.foxnews.com/us/texas-court-orders-kaitlin-armstrong-pay-murder-victims-family) on 17 June 2024.
- **Fraudulent transfer.** In July 2024 the Wilsons [sued Armstrong, her mother, her sister and Strickland](https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/family-of-mo-wilson-suing-kaitlin-armstrong-and-family-for-fraudulent-transfer), alleging that her property and money had been moved out of her name to avoid paying them. These are allegations; we have not found any ruling on them.

In 2023, [FOX 7 reported](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/kaitlin-armstrong-escape-charges-dismissed) that a federal charge over the use of her sister's passport was still possible. We have found no report that one has been brought.

## Moriah's family, friends and legacy

Moriah's family has spent four years insisting that she be remembered for how she lived. Her brother Matt graduated from Middlebury College two weeks after her death and went straight into setting up the [Moriah Wilson Foundation](https://velo.outsideonline.com/gravel/the-moriah-wilson-foundation-using-momentum-and-more-to-honor-the-late-cyclists-legacy/), whose mission is to promote healthy living and community by supporting organisations that widen access to recreation, sport and education. It began close to home, with youth projects in Vermont's Northeast Kingdom.

"She inspired me my whole life to believe in myself, push myself, work hard," he said. "The foundation for me is about sharing the way she inspired me and our family and all of our friends and everyone she touched."

The cycling world has kept her name in the sport. Every May the foundation holds the [Ride for Mo](https://www.cyclingnews.com/news/ride-for-mo-invites-cycling-community-to-honour-moriah-wilson) on the roads and trails around Lyndonville, Vermont, where she grew up; the family describes it as "not a race but a day to join Moriah's spirit on the roads and trails she grew up on and loved." Her friend Caitlin Cash, who found her, spoke at the sentencing, and her mother Karen has since talked publicly about grief and faith. The family's statement on the documentary sums up what they took from the trial: "integrity matters. Our words matter, our actions matter, and above all, how we love matters."

## Netflix's The Truth and Tragedy of Moriah Wilson: what the documentary covers

[*The Truth and Tragedy of Moriah Wilson*](https://www.netflix.com/title/81763772) is a feature-length Netflix documentary, not a series. It was directed by Marina Zenovich, who won Emmys for *Roman Polanski: Wanted and Desired* and made ESPN's *Lance* about Lance Armstrong (no relation to Kaitlin), and produced by Evan Hayes of the Oscar-winning *Free Solo* for [Unreasonable Studios](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/the-truth-and-tragedy-of-moriah-wilson-trailer-and-release-date-set-for-netflix-doc-premiering-at-sxsw/), with a score by Marco Beltrami.

Netflix describes it as "an intimate, arresting portrait of cyclist Moriah Wilson — raised by a fierce, loving family — whose singular drive becomes her superpower." Based on Netflix's material and interviews with the filmmakers:

- **It starts with Mo's life.** Hayes told [FOX 7](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/sxsw-2026-documentary-about-moriah-wilson-premiers-austin) that the family shared home videos, race footage and Moriah's own diary entries, including "footage of her as a baby and her on skis for the very first time".
- **The people who loved her tell the story.** Interviews with her family, friends and fellow cyclists run through the film. Hayes said he spent time with the Wilsons during the trial, and that this "provided a point of view that was totally different from what everybody else was experiencing".
- **It uses the case evidence.** The [Statesman reported](https://www.statesman.com/news/article/moriah-wilson-murder-documentary-netflix-austin-22091807.php) that it includes police body camera footage, surveillance video and interrogation clips involving Colin Strickland.
- **The family helped but did not control it.** Matt Wilson told *The Dartmouth* the family didn't have "much participation in the actual making" of the film, but gathered photos and videos and put the producers in touch with people. Royalties from the film go to the Moriah Wilson Foundation.

## Is The Truth and Tragedy of Moriah Wilson worth watching?

**Yes, especially if you only know the case from the headlines. Less so if you want new evidence or a forensic reinvestigation.**

<Callout type="verdict">
This is a film about a person first and a case second, and that is its strength. Its makers set out to show who Mo Wilson was before anyone outside cycling had heard her name, and the family's home videos and her own writing give it something no previous treatment of the case has had. It is not a reinvestigation: the facts were settled at trial, and the film doesn't pretend otherwise. Watch it to understand what was lost. Then use the podcasts below for the evidence in detail.
</Callout>

**What it does particularly well:**

- **It gives Mo her story back.** Her brother said the family felt she "didn't have any say in who she was" amid coverage built around a "love triangle". The film makes her the protagonist, which is exactly what most earlier coverage failed to do.
- **It shows the people left behind.** The *Wall Street Journal*'s John Anderson noted that each painful moment "is counterbalanced by another dazzling photo of the cyclist's smiling face". *Decider* called it "as much a remembrance and celebration of a life as it is a ride into the world of true crime".
- **It is more restrained than most of the genre.** Critic Stephen Silver called it "a considerably more tasteful true crime documentary than most of what's on offer in that genre on Netflix".

**What to keep in mind:**

- **Not every critic was persuaded.** The *New York Times*' Ben Kenigsberg wrote: "If there's anything illuminating or edifying about watching these painful recollections, 'Truth and Tragedy' misses it." Valerie Kalfrin of AWFJ found it "sadly familiar, with creative choices that dull some of the emotional impact".
- **It is told from one side, by design.** It is built around the Wilsons' experience. That is a fair choice for a film about Moriah, but it means the trial arguments get less space than a courtroom podcast would give them.
- **It is short.** At around 90 minutes it has to compress a 43-day manhunt and a three-week trial. If you finish it wanting more detail, that's what the listening guide below is for.

## Why the Moriah Wilson case still matters

**It shows how coverage can erase a victim.** Within days, the story had become a "love triangle", with Moriah as one corner of it. Her family's long effort to change that, which the Netflix film is part of, is a reminder of who true crime stories are really about. Our piece on [the ethics of true crime podcasting](/blog/ethics-of-true-crime-podcasting) looks at where coverage helps families and where it crosses a line.

**Modern evidence did the work.** No one saw the shooting. The case was built from a car's GPS logs, a doorbell camera, phone data, ballistics and DNA, and on appeal the court rejected the challenge to its most novel element, the "activity level" DNA evidence.

**The 43 days.** Armstrong walked out of a police station the day after the killing. How she was able to leave the country before the murder warrant was issued, and how she was found, is the part of the case most people remember, and the part the Marshals and the Diplomatic Security Service have talked about most.

## The bottom line

Moriah Wilson was 25, a week from her 26th birthday, and on her way to being one of the best bike racers in the world when she was shot dead in Austin on 11 May 2022. Kaitlin Armstrong was convicted of her murder in 2023 and sentenced to 90 years, and in 2026 the Texas courts rejected her appeal. Netflix's *The Truth and Tragedy of Moriah Wilson* is the first telling of the story built around Mo herself, and the best way to follow it up is to hear her voice on The Gravel Ride before you go back to the case.
