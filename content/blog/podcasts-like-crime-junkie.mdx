Crime Junkie works because you always know what you're getting. Every Monday, Ashley Flowers tells her best friend Brit Prawat one case from start to finish (a murder, a disappearance, a mysterious death), with the victim at the centre, in a little under an hour. If you've caught up and want more, the best podcasts like Crime Junkie keep that clarity and pick one thing to do differently.

**Short answer:** for the same feel, try [Anatomy of Murder](#1-anatomy-of-murder) or [The Deck](#2-the-deck), both made by Crime Junkie's own company, audiochuck. For two friends talking through a case, try [Morbid](#5-morbid). For more depth, try [Casefile](#7-casefile-true-crime). For a whole season on one case, try [Bear Brook](#13-bear-brook).

We picked these 15 for how close they are to Crime Junkie, not for how famous they are. Episode lengths and release days come from each show's own podcast feed, checked in November 2026.

## What Crime Junkie fans usually want next

Most people who search for a podcast like Crime Junkie want more of one of these:

- **The format:** one case per episode, explained from scratch, in under an hour.
- **The hosts:** two friends, one telling the story and one reacting.
- **The focus on victims:** the person who was lost, the family, and the push for an answer.
- **The missing-person and cold-case episodes:** the ones that end with a call for information.

The sections below follow that order. If you're not sure where to start, begin with the first section.

## Closest to Crime Junkie: same company, same format

These four shows come from audiochuck, the company Ashley Flowers set up to make Crime Junkie. They have the same production style and the same focus on victims, and each one adds a different angle.

### 1. Anatomy of Murder

**What it covers:** homicide cases told from the investigation through to the courtroom. Former homicide prosecutor Anna-Sigga Nicolazzi and investigative journalist Scott Weinberger host, and every episode includes someone directly involved: a victim's relative, the prosecutor or a detective on the case.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it's the same company, the same pacing and the same respect for victims. You get one case per episode, told clearly.

**How it's different:** there's less friendly chat and more procedure. Where Crime Junkie ends with what happened, Anatomy of Murder spends time on how the case was proved.

**Format:** one case per episode, usually 40 to 50 minutes. There are more than 250 episodes, but the feed's most recent new episode is from January 2026, so for now think of it as a back catalogue rather than a weekly show.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/anatomy-of-murder/id1532894981) or any podcast app.

### 2. The Deck

**What it covers:** some police forces print photos of missing and murdered people on decks of playing cards and hand them out in prisons, hoping someone inside knows something. Each episode of The Deck takes one of those cards and goes through the case with investigators and family members.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** Ashley Flowers hosts it, and each episode ends the way the best Crime Junkie cold-case episodes do, by asking listeners for information.

**How it's different:** Ashley tells the story alone, without a co-host, and the episodes are shorter and focused only on unsolved cases.

**Format:** one cold case per episode, usually 30 to 40 minutes, released on Wednesdays. There are 250 episodes since January 2022.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-deck/id1603011962) or any podcast app.

### 3. Park Predators

**What it covers:** murders and disappearances in national parks and forests. Investigative journalist Delia D'Ambra digs through archives and visits the places where each case happened.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it's the same company and the same tight, victim-first storytelling, with a theme that runs through every episode.

**How it's different:** there's one narrator instead of a conversation, and the setting matters as much as the crime.

**Format:** one case per episode, usually 30 to 40 minutes, released on Tuesdays. There are about 185 episodes since 2020.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/park-predators/id1517651197) or any podcast app.

### 4. Dark Downeast

**What it covers:** unsolved killings, unexplained deaths and disappearances in Maine and the rest of New England. Investigative journalist Kylie Low narrates, with interviews with families, friends and detectives.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it has the same careful, respectful approach to victims, and it covers cases that bigger shows rarely reach.

**How it's different:** it covers one region, uses more interviews, and splits bigger cases into two parts.

**Format:** usually 40 to 45 minutes, released on Thursdays. There are nearly 300 episodes since 2020.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/dark-downeast/id1535403320) or any podcast app.

## If you love the best-friend chat

### 5. Morbid

**What it covers:** true crime, creepy history and the occasional ghost story. It's hosted by autopsy technician Alaina Urquhart and hairstylist Ash Kelley.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** if Ashley and Brit's friendship is half the appeal for you, Morbid gives you that and more. Alaina's job also brings real knowledge to the forensic details.

**How it's different:** it's funnier and wanders off topic more, and not every episode is about crime. Some listeners love the jokes; if you want the story straight, start further up this list.

**Format:** about 50 to 70 minutes, released twice a week (usually Mondays and Thursdays). There are more than 900 episodes since 2018, and it's ad-free on SiriusXM Podcasts+.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/morbid/id1379959217) or any podcast app.

### 6. RedHanded

**What it covers:** serial killers, strange mysteries, hauntings and plenty of cases from the UK and beyond. It's hosted by London-based friends Suruthi Bala and Hannah Maguire.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it's the closest British version of the Crime Junkie set-up: two friends, with one telling the other a case.

**How it's different:** it's more irreverent and covers a wider range, including the unexplained. The hosts also comment more on the social side of each case.

**Format:** full-length episodes (usually about an hour, sometimes in two parts) plus shorter "ShortHand" episodes of around 30 minutes. There are several releases a week and nearly 700 episodes since 2017.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/redhanded/id1250599915) or any podcast app. For more UK shows, see our guide to the [best British true crime podcasts](/blog/best-british-true-crime-podcasts).

## If you want more detail per case

### 7. Casefile True Crime

**What it covers:** one case at a time from Australia and around the world, read by an anonymous narrator known only as "the host".

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it's one of the most common next steps for Crime Junkie listeners. It has the same one-case structure, but each case is told in far more detail.

**How it's different:** there's no co-host, no banter and no opinion, just a calm, carefully researched script. Episodes are much longer, and big cases run to several parts.

**Format:** usually 70 to 100 minutes, mostly released on Saturdays. There are more than 350 cases since 2016, and Casefile Premium gives you ad-free episodes a week early.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/casefile-true-crime/id998568017) or the show's site, [casefilepodcast.com](https://casefilepodcast.com). ### 8. Canadian True Crime

**What it covers:** Canadian cases, from small-town murders to big-city investigations. It's researched and narrated by Kristi Lee, who describes her approach as trauma-informed.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it has the same victim-focused approach, and almost none of the cases overlap with American shows, so it all feels new.

**How it's different:** it's a single narrator, with longer episodes and more background on the wider context of each case. It's also an independent production.

**Format:** usually 60 to 80 minutes. New episodes come out regularly, but not on a fixed day. There are about 240 episodes since 2017, plus ad-free premium feeds on Amazon Music (included with Prime), Apple Podcasts and Patreon.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/canadian-true-crime/id1197095887) or [canadiantruecrime.ca](https://www.canadiantruecrime.ca).

### 9. Small Town Dicks

**What it covers:** small-town cases told by the twin detectives who actually worked them, Dan and Dave. Yeardley Smith hosts, and forensic investigator Paul Holes joins the panel.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** you get the inside story of how a case was solved, from the people who solved it.

**How it's different:** it's a conversation led by the investigators, not a narrated retelling, so it focuses more on police work and less on the wider story.

**Format:** usually 50 to 65 minutes, released on Fridays. There are about 300 episodes since 2017.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/small-town-dicks/id1271261548) or [smalltowndicks.com](https://www.smalltowndicks.com).

## If the missing-person episodes are your favourites

### 10. The Vanished

**What it covers:** one missing person per episode. Host Marissa Jones interviews the people who knew them, along with investigators and experts.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** if Crime Junkie's "MISSING:" episodes are the ones you save for last, this whole show is that. Most cases are still open, and the show doesn't pretend otherwise.

**How it's different:** it's built around interviews with families rather than one host telling the story, and most episodes don't have an ending.

**Format:** about an hour, released on Mondays. There are more than 550 episodes since 2016. It's now an Audible Original, and Audible members can listen ad-free.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-vanished-podcast/id1081124929) or any podcast app. For more, see our guide to the [best missing persons podcasts](/blog/best-missing-persons-podcasts).

### 11. Hell and Gone

**What it covers:** unsolved deaths that journalist and private investigator Catherine Townsend investigates herself, often after families contact her for help.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it has the same goal of getting cases solved, but here you hear the investigation as it happens.

**How it's different:** the host is part of the story, chasing leads in person rather than retelling a case that's already been reported.

**Format:** seasons of episodes around 30 to 40 minutes. The current fifth season, *Murder Line*, is weekly.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/hell-and-gone-murder-line/id1437808691) or any podcast app.

## Ready for one case over a whole season?

These shows stop doing one case per episode. Each season follows one case. They're the natural next step once you want more than an hour on a story. For more like this, see our guides to [podcasts like Serial](/blog/podcasts-like-serial) and the [best investigative journalism podcasts](/blog/best-investigative-journalism-podcasts).

### 12. Up and Vanished

**What it covers:** Payne Lindsey's documentary series, starting with season 1 on the 2005 disappearance of Georgia teacher Tara Grinstead. Later seasons take on other disappearances.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it feels like a long Crime Junkie "MISSING:" episode that keeps going: easy to follow, with plenty of cliffhangers.

**How it's different:** it's a reporter on the ground following leads, so the story unfolds as he reports it.

**Format:** seasons of episodes around 30 to 40 minutes, with nearly 200 episodes in the feed since 2016. Start at season 1, episode 1.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/up-and-vanished/id1140596919) or any podcast app.

### 13. Bear Brook

**What it covers:** New Hampshire Public Radio's Jason Moon on a cold case involving four bodies found in barrels in the New Hampshire woods (season 1), and on a man serving life for a murder he says he didn't commit (season 2). Season 3, *A Private Matter*, started in October 2026 and follows a Christmas tree farmer whose wife never reported him missing.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** season 1 is about how investigators gave names to unidentified victims, the kind of story Crime Junkie listeners love.

**How it's different:** it's public-radio reporting, slower and more careful, and it covers the forensic science in detail.

**Format:** three short seasons, with episodes of about 35 to 50 minutes. There are 28 episodes in total, which you could finish in a long weekend.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/bear-brook/id1423306695) or any podcast app.

### 14. Your Own Backyard

**What it covers:** Chris Lambert's independent investigation into the 1996 disappearance of Cal Poly student Kristin Smart. It follows the case through to the trial and conviction of Paul Flores for her murder in 2022.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it takes the kind of missing-person case Crime Junkie would cover in one episode and stays with it for 29, without losing sight of Kristin and her family.

**How it's different:** it's one case followed for years by someone who lives in the community.

**Format:** 29 episodes of about 50 to 70 minutes. It's complete, with the last episode released in 2023.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/your-own-backyard/id1480263708) or [yourownbackyardpodcast.com](https://yourownbackyardpodcast.com).

### 15. The Teacher's Pet

**What it covers:** Hedley Thomas of *The Australian* re-investigates the 1982 disappearance of Lynette Dawson from Sydney's Northern Beaches. Her husband, Chris Dawson, was convicted of her murder in 2022.

**Why Crime Junkie fans like it:** it has the same focus on a woman who was overlooked for decades, told as one long story with a clear ending.

**How it's different:** it's newspaper investigative journalism. It's Australian and covers police and legal failures in depth.

**Format:** about 30 episodes in the feed, including later updates, mostly 30 to 70 minutes each.

**Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-teachers-pet/id1385379989) or any podcast app.

## Which one should you start with?

- **You want more Crime Junkie:** start with Anatomy of Murder, then The Deck.
- **You want shorter episodes:** start with The Deck or Park Predators (both about 30 to 40 minutes). There are more in our guide to [short true crime podcasts](/blog/best-short-true-crime-podcasts).
- **You want the friendship and humour:** start with Morbid, or RedHanded if you'd like British hosts. Our [podcasts like My Favorite Murder](/blog/podcasts-like-my-favorite-murder) list has more chatty two-host shows.
- **You want more depth:** start with Casefile, then Canadian True Crime.
- **You want one case over a whole season:** start with Bear Brook, then Your Own Backyard.

Most listeners end up with both: a weekly show for the commute, and one longer series for the weekend.
