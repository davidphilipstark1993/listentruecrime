Almost every major true crime podcast is on Spotify, so the question isn't what is available but what is worth your time. These nine are our picks: five of the most popular shows on the platform, one Parcast pick, and three excellent series that Spotify's recommendations rarely surface. Every one was available on Spotify when we last checked.

Spotify's strength is suggesting shows similar to ones you already play. Its weakness is that it favours what is already popular. This guide is meant to fill that gap.

For our overall ranking, whatever app you use, see the [best true crime podcasts of 2026](/best-true-crime-podcasts).

## The Best Established Shows Available on Spotify

All of the following are freely available on Spotify alongside other podcast platforms.

### Crime Junkie — The Most Popular Weekly Show

**Crime Junkie** is regularly near the top of Spotify's true crime charts. Ashley Flowers and Brit Prawat cover murders, disappearances and cold cases in episodes of about 30 to 45 minutes, with a focus on victims and unsolved cases.

It is one of the easiest starting points on Spotify: the format is simple to follow and the episode length suits a commute. If you're completely new, our [beginner's guide](/blog/best-true-crime-podcasts-for-beginners) explains how it compares with other styles.

[Browse Crime Junkie →](/podcasts/crime-junkie)

### Casefile — The Best Produced Narrative True Crime

**Casefile** is the most consistently excellent narrative true crime podcast available on any platform. The anonymous Australian host delivers meticulously researched cases with exceptional production quality.

It's a different experience to Crime Junkie — longer episodes, more cinematic, more emotionally demanding — but if you want the best storytelling in the genre, this is it.

[Browse Casefile →](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime)

### Morbid — The Best Community-Focused Show

**Morbid** is hosted by Alaina Urquhart, an autopsy technician, and her niece Ash Kelley. It mixes forensic knowledge with family banter and dark humour, and covers historical cases and weird history as well as murder.

What distinguishes it: Urquhart's work in autopsy gives the forensic parts of each case more substance than most chat-style shows.

[Browse Morbid →](/podcasts/morbid)

### My Favorite Murder — The Entry Point for Comedy True Crime

**My Favorite Murder** with Karen Kilgariff and Georgia Hardstark helped create the comedy true crime format. Warm, funny and less formally researched than a documentary series. A good starting point if you aren't sure you want something heavy. More in the same style is in our [comedy true crime guide](/blog/best-comedy-true-crime-podcasts).

[Browse My Favorite Murder →](/podcasts/my-favorite-murder)

### Serial — Still the Best Starting Point

**Serial** is available on Spotify and remains one of the finest podcasts ever made. Season 1 — the investigation of Adnan Syed's conviction for the murder of Hae Min Lee — is required listening for anyone interested in the genre.

[Browse Serial →](/podcasts/serial)

## Spotify Originals and Parcast Content

Parcast, bought by Spotify in 2019, makes a large catalogue of scripted true crime shows, including **[Serial Killers](/podcasts/serial-killers)** and **[Cults](/podcasts/cults)**. They are professionally produced and easy to follow, but they retell cases from existing sources rather than reporting new material. Some began as Spotify-only releases and are now in other apps too.

If you want one Parcast show, make it Cults, which is the first pick in our [best cult podcasts guide](/blog/best-cult-podcasts) for listeners who want variety.

For fraud and white-collar crime, try **Swindled**, an independent show (not a Parcast production) told by an anonymous host with a dry, satirical voice. Our [fraud and scam podcasts guide](/blog/best-fraud-scam-podcasts) has more.

## How to Discover True Crime on Spotify

Spotify's recommendations get better the more you listen. Once it knows what you like, it is good at suggesting similar shows.

**Practical tips:**

1. **Follow the shows you like.** Spotify uses what you follow and finish to shape its suggestions, so following a few good shows quickly improves them.
2. **Check the show page's suggestions.** Each show page suggests similar podcasts, which works well once you know which style you prefer.
3. **Look beyond the charts.** The True Crime chart shows what is popular this week, not what is best. The three shows below rarely appear there.
4. **Use ListenTrueCrime for reviews.** Spotify listings don't include detailed reviews. Our [podcast directory](/browse) has a review and binge score for every show, plus where to listen.

## The Shows Spotify's Algorithm Won't Surface

The algorithmic recommendation problem: Spotify surfaces what's popular, not necessarily what's best. Some exceptional podcasts with smaller audiences get consistently under-recommended.

**The Trail Went Cold** — a Canadian-hosted show covering unsolved cases from around the world in short, carefully researched episodes. It has a much smaller audience than Crime Junkie, so Spotify rarely suggests it.

**Hunting Warhead** (CBC Podcasts and the Norwegian newspaper VG) — an investigation into a dark-web child abuse site and the people who brought it down. Extremely difficult subject matter, but outstanding journalism.

**Missing & Murdered: Finding Cleo** (CBC) — Connie Walker's search for a Cree girl taken from her family in the Sixties Scoop. Award-winning, careful reporting.

Both feature in our [best investigative podcasts](/blog/best-investigative-journalism-podcasts) guide.

## Platform Comparison: Spotify vs Apple Podcasts for True Crime

| Feature | Spotify | Apple Podcasts |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogue size | Comprehensive | Comprehensive |
| Exclusive content | Very little now | Very little |
| Discovery/recommendations | Strong, favours popular shows | Good |
| Cross-device sync | Any device | Best on Apple devices |
| Offline listening | Yes (free) | Yes (free) |
| User interface | Modern, intuitive | Reliable, clean |

Both platforms have essentially the same catalogue for major shows. The choice between them is largely a matter of which ecosystem you prefer.
