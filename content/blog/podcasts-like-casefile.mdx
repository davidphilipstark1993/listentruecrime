**Quick answer:** the closest podcasts to Casefile are **Canadian True Crime** (same scripted, single-narrator anthology format), **Murder Mile UK True Crime** (solo, documentary-style, British cases) and **Unresolved** (long, carefully sourced cold-case episodes). For Australian cases, start with **The Teacher's Pet** and **The Night Driver**. If you want one story told over a whole season, try **Bear Brook**, **Cold** or **West Cork**.

Casefile has a style that's easy to recognise: an anonymous Australian narrator, a full script, no small talk, and cases from Australia, the UK and the US given hour-plus episodes. No other show does all of that at once. So the list below is grouped by the part of Casefile you most want more of. Each pick says what matches and what doesn't.

Our [Casefile podcast profile](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime) covers the show itself, including which episodes to start with.

**Anthology** shows cover a new case every episode or two, like most of Casefile's catalogue. **Single case** shows spend a whole series on one story, like Casefile's longer multi-part cases.

## Closest in style: scripted, single-narrator anthologies

These five are the nearest thing to Casefile's weekly format: one narrator, a script, one case at a time.

### 1. Canadian True Crime

Kristi Lee researches and narrates each case herself, working from court records, inquiry reports and news coverage, and the show publishes a source list for every episode on its website. There's no co-host and no chat, and the delivery is steady and measured, much like Casefile's. The main differences are that Lee is a named host and the show sticks to Canadian cases. Casefile listeners who like its UK and Australian episodes for showing a different justice system will probably get the same thing here. Episodes typically run 45–60 minutes.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="canadian-true-crime" title="Canadian True Crime" description="Kristi Lee narrates scripted, sourced episodes on Canadian cases. The closest match to Casefile's format, with a named host and a single-country focus." tags={["Solo narrator", "Anthology", "Canada"]} />

### 2. Murder Mile UK True Crime

Michael J. Buchanan-Dunne writes, researches and narrates every episode himself. Each case is tied to the London streets where it happened, and the series grew out of his Soho murder walking tours. It's a solo, documentary-style show with no banter, and there are hundreds of episodes in the back catalogue. If you like Casefile's British episodes, start here. The tone is a little more atmospheric than Casefile's, and every case is from London.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="murder-mile-uk-true-crime" title="Murder Mile UK True Crime" description="Solo-narrated London murders, researched and written by Michael J. Buchanan-Dunne. A strong pick for fans of Casefile's UK cases." tags={["Solo narrator", "Anthology", "UK"]} />

### 3. Unresolved

Micheal Whelan has been making Unresolved since 2015, and it is closer to Casefile than almost anything else on research depth. Episodes are long, cases sometimes stretch across several parts, and the show doesn't force a neat answer when the evidence doesn't support one. It leans further towards cold cases and unexplained disappearances than Casefile does, so it suits listeners who like Casefile's unsolved episodes best. Releases are less regular than Casefile's weekly schedule.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="unresolved" title="Unresolved" description="Long, carefully researched episodes on cold cases and unexplained disappearances. Ideal if Casefile's unsolved episodes are your favourites." tags={["Cold Case", "Anthology", "Unsolved"]} />

### 4. Swindled

Swindled is the best-known show besides Casefile with an anonymous narrator. Its host goes only by "A Concerned Citizen" and reads a script with no guests or chat. The subject matter is different: corporate fraud, Ponzi schemes and white-collar crime rather than murder. The tone is also different, because the narrator is openly sarcastic about the people involved. Try it if Casefile's anonymous, script-only format is what you like most and you want a break from violent crime.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="swindled" title="Swindled" description="An anonymous narrator covers white-collar crime in scripted episodes. The same format as Casefile, but a drier tone and very different cases." tags={["Anonymous host", "White-Collar Crime", "Anthology"]} />

### 5. Criminal

Phoebe Judge is a calm, restrained host, and the show never sensationalises its subjects, which will feel familiar to Casefile listeners. But Criminal is broader and shorter. Many episodes are under 30 minutes, and the stories range from murders to art theft and odd legal footnotes. Treat it as a lighter companion to Casefile for when you don't have time for a 90-minute case, not as a replacement.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="criminal" title="Criminal" description="Phoebe Judge's calm anthology of crime stories. Restrained like Casefile but shorter and broader, so it works well between longer cases." tags={["Anthology", "Short Episodes", "Narrative"]} />

## Australian true crime podcasts for Casefile fans

Many people find Casefile through its Australian cases. These four are Australian investigations, but each one follows a single case across a whole series, so they're closer to Casefile's multi-part cases than to its standalone episodes. Our [guide to the best Australian true crime podcasts](/blog/best-australian-true-crime-podcasts) covers more.

### 6. The Teacher's Pet

Journalist Hedley Thomas of The Australian reinvestigated the 1982 disappearance of Sydney mother Lynette Dawson. Her husband, Chris Dawson, was arrested in December 2018 and convicted of her murder in 2022. The podcast is widely credited with getting the case reopened. It was pulled from Australian download in 2019 ahead of the trial and was made available again after the verdict. Thomas is a much more visible presence than Casefile's narrator, but the subject and the level of detail are just as serious.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="the-teachers-pet" title="The Teacher's Pet" description="Hedley Thomas reinvestigates Lynette Dawson's 1982 disappearance. One of the most consequential Australian true crime podcasts." tags={["Australia", "Single-Case", "Investigative"]} />

### 7. The Night Driver

In The Night Driver, Hedley Thomas looks into the 2001 disappearance of Janine Vaughan, who was seen getting into a red car in Bathurst, New South Wales, and never seen again. It takes the same approach as The Teacher's Pet: a long, patient investigation into a case police never solved, set in a town still divided by rumour. If you liked The Teacher's Pet, listen to this next.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="the-night-driver" title="The Night Driver" description="Hedley Thomas investigates the 2001 disappearance of Janine Vaughan in Bathurst, NSW. Serious, long-form Australian reporting." tags={["Australia", "Missing Person", "Single-Case"]} />

### 8. The Lady Vanishes

In 1997, schoolteacher Marion Barter told her family she was going to the UK and was never seen again. Her daughter, Sally Leydon, has spent decades searching for her, and the podcast follows that search. It helped push New South Wales Police to review the case, which led to a coronial inquest. The family's involvement makes it more personal than Casefile, but it's a useful example of a case that went cold being reopened.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="the-lady-vanishes" title="The Lady Vanishes" description="The search for Marion Barter, who disappeared in 1997. The podcast helped push NSW Police to review the case, which led to a coronial inquest." tags={["Australia", "Cold Case", "Missing Person"]} />

### 9. Lost in Larrimah

In 2017, Paddy Moriarty and his dog disappeared from Larrimah, a Northern Territory town with only about a dozen residents. The town's feuding locals soon became the obvious suspects. This is the least Casefile-like pick here in tone, because the hosts allow some dry humour about the town. It's included because few podcasts capture remote Australia this well, and no one has ever been charged over his disappearance.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="lost-in-larrimah" title="Lost in Larrimah" description="A disappearance in a tiny outback town. Lighter in tone than Casefile but a vivid Australian unsolved case." tags={["Australia", "Unsolved", "Single-Case"]} />

## Multi-episode investigations for Casefile fans

Casefile's best-known cases often run to several parts. If that's when you enjoy it most, these documentary series spend a whole season on one case.

### 10. Bear Brook

Four unidentified bodies were found in barrels in a New Hampshire state park, and the case went unsolved for decades. Jason Moon of NHPR tells the story from the original discovery to the genetic genealogy work that finally cracked it. It's careful, unhurried and full of forensic detail, much like Casefile, though Moon is more present as a reporter. You can read more about the techniques in our [genetic genealogy explainer](/blog/genetic-genealogy-cold-cases).

<PodcastRecommendation slug="bear-brook" title="Bear Brook" description="Unidentified victims, a decades-old cold case and the DNA genealogy work that finally solved it. Patient and detailed in the same way as Casefile." tags={["Cold Case", "Forensics", "Single-Case"]} />

### 11. Cold

KSL reporter Dave Cawley built Season 1 around the 2009 disappearance of Utah mother Susan Powell. He drew on thousands of pages of police files and Susan's own journals. Later seasons follow other Utah cases in the same document-heavy way. It's the closest single-case series here to Casefile's evidence-first approach.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="cold" title="Cold" description="Dave Cawley works through police files on the Susan Powell disappearance and other Utah cases. Strongly evidence-led." tags={["Missing Person", "Investigative", "Single-Case"]} />

### 12. West Cork

Sam Bungey and Jennifer Forde spent years on the 1996 murder of Sophie Toscan du Plantier at her holiday home in rural Ireland. The series looks at the crime, the main suspect, and a community that couldn't agree on what happened. It started as an Audible exclusive and has been available free on the main podcast apps since 2021. If you like Casefile's European episodes, this is one of the best single-case series on the list.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="west-cork" title="West Cork" description="The 1996 murder of Sophie Toscan du Plantier in rural Ireland, told over 13 episodes. Long form and thoroughly reported." tags={["Ireland", "Murder", "Single-Case"]} />

### 13. In the Dark

In the Dark is a journalism show first and a true crime show second. Season 2 spent a year on Curtis Flowers, who was tried six times for the same murders. The US Supreme Court overturned his conviction in 2019, and the charges were later dropped. The research is as thorough as anything in the genre. Expect a named reporter and a focus on how the system failed, not just on the crime itself.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="in-the-dark" title="In the Dark" description="Journalism-grade investigations into how cases go wrong. Matches Casefile on research depth, with a named reporter." tags={["Investigative", "Wrongful Conviction", "Journalism"]} />

### 14. Bone Valley

Pulitzer Prize-winning author Gilbert King looks at the case of Leo Schofield, convicted of the 1987 murder of his wife Michelle in Florida. Years later, a fingerprint at the scene was matched to someone else. The nine episodes are built on case files and court records, and the narration is measured. Casefile listeners who like the episodes about investigations going wrong will find it very rewarding.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="bone-valley" title="Bone Valley" description="Gilbert King re-examines a 1987 Florida murder conviction over nine evidence-driven episodes." tags={["Wrongful Conviction", "Single-Case", "Investigative"]} />

### 15. Your Own Backyard

Chris Lambert, a musician with no background in journalism, spent years on the 1996 disappearance of Cal Poly student Kristin Smart. The podcast is credited with bringing out new witnesses, and Paul Flores was convicted of her murder in 2022. It's rougher and much more personal than Casefile, but it shows how far persistent research can take a cold case.

<PodcastRecommendation slug="your-own-backyard" title="Your Own Backyard" description="An amateur investigator's years-long work on the Kristin Smart case, which helped bring about a murder conviction 25 years later." tags={["Cold Case", "Single-Case", "Investigative"]} />

## Popular picks that are less similar to Casefile

Some shows come up often in Casefile recommendation threads but don't really match what Casefile does:

- **[Serial](/podcasts/serial)** has the research depth and is worth hearing, but it's a personal, first-person investigation, very different from Casefile's anonymous narrator. Our [Serial vs Casefile comparison](/blog/serial-vs-casefile) covers the difference.
- **[Crime Junkie](/podcasts/crime-junkie)** and **[RedHanded](/podcasts/redhanded)** are good hosted shows, but they're built around the hosts' conversation and personalities. That's the opposite of Casefile's appeal. See our [Crime Junkie vs Casefile vs My Favorite Murder](/blog/crime-junkie-vs-casefile-vs-my-favorite-murder) breakdown.
- **[Australian True Crime](/podcasts/australian-true-crime-meshel-laurie)** covers the right cases, but it's built on interviews with reporters, detectives and families rather than scripted narration.

## Where to start

- **You want something that feels like Casefile:** start with Canadian True Crime.
- **You want more Australian cases:** start with The Teacher's Pet, then The Night Driver.
- **You like Casefile's unsolved episodes most:** start with Unresolved, then Bear Brook.
- **You want one story told over a whole season:** start with West Cork or Cold.
