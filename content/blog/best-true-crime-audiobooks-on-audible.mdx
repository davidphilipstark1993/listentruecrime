If you already spend hours inside serialised true crime, an audiobook is the natural next step. The format is the same: one voice, a long-running story, something to do with your hands while you listen. What changes is the depth. A podcast series gives you six to twelve hours at most. A good true crime book can carry the research of years, and it can correct itself, cite its sources and give the people at the centre of a case more room than an episode can.

This guide is for people who listen first and read second. Each of the seven audiobooks below is matched to a podcast on the site that covers the same ground, so you can move between formats without repeating yourself. Everything here is available on Audible through Amazon UK, and where we say so, we have checked that there is an audiobook edition rather than assuming one exists.

## What makes a good true crime audiobook

Not every strong book works on audio, and not every audiobook is a good listen. The ones that work tend to share three traits.

**A clear narrative thread.** Audio is linear. You cannot flip back to check who someone is, so books built around a single case, investigation or person travel far better than sprawling histories with large casts.

**Names and dates that are easy to follow by ear.** Books that lean heavily on tables, timelines or long lists of suspects are better read than heard. The books below mostly tell a story in sequence.

**Subject matter you are happy to spend a long time with.** An audiobook is a commitment of many hours. Pick a case you are curious about rather than one you feel you ought to know.

One practical note: the listing on Amazon UK shows the running length and the narrator, so check both before you decide. We are deliberately not quoting either here.

## If you want a case investigated

### I'll Be Gone in the Dark — Michelle McNamara

McNamara spent years investigating the unidentified offender she named the Golden State Killer. She died in 2016 before finishing the book. It was completed by others and published in 2018, shortly before the arrest that made the case's resolution public.

It is the most natural audiobook for a podcast listener because it works the way the best podcasts do: part case file, part personal obsession, with the investigator herself on the page. **Pair it with** [Man in the Window: The Golden State Killer](/podcasts/man-in-the-window-golden-state-killer), a serialised podcast that tells the case through the victims and rates a binge factor of 8 on our scale, or with [Jensen & Holes: The Murder Squad](/podcasts/the-murder-squad), rated a **Must listen**, which follows Paul Holes, the investigator who worked the case. We cover the case itself on our [Golden State Killer case page](/cases/golden-state-killer).

### American Predator — Maureen Callahan

Callahan's book follows Israel Keyes, the serial killer caught in 2012 after the murder of Samantha Koenig. What makes it unusual is how little of it is about the capture and how much is about the years of careful planning that came before, and the investigators who worked to understand him afterwards.

It is tightly structured and linear, which suits audio well. **Pair it with** the [American Predator podcast](/podcasts/american-predator), a scripted, single-narrator series on the same case that we rate **Worth a listen**, with a binge factor of 7. If you like the restraint of Casefile, our [podcasts like Casefile](/blog/podcasts-like-casefile) guide explains why this one sits so close to it.

## If you want to understand the offender

### The Stranger Beside Me — Ann Rule

Rule was a crime writer who had worked alongside Ted Bundy before he was identified as a suspect, and the book is as much about how someone close to an offender fails to see him as it is about the crimes. It remains one of the most widely read accounts of the Bundy case, and it is long.

If you would rather start with something shorter, [The Bundy Tapes](/podcasts/the-bundy-tapes) is a Netflix documentary series built on Bundy's own recorded conversations, rated by us as good if US true crime interests you. For the wider field, see our guide to the [best serial killer podcasts](/blog/best-serial-killer-podcasts).

## If you want the scandal

### Bad Blood — John Carreyrou

Carreyrou is the Wall Street Journal reporter who exposed Theranos, and *Bad Blood* is his account of the fraud and the efforts to stop his reporting. It reads like a thriller because the reporting was built to be followed step by step.

**Pair it with** [The Dropout](/podcasts/the-dropout), a serialised ABC podcast rated a **Must listen**, with a binge factor of 8 and eight episodes of roughly 30 to 45 minutes. The two complement each other: the podcast is the faster route in, and the book is where the detail lives. For more on the case, see our [Theranos case page](/cases/theranos-elizabeth-holmes) and our guide to the [best fraud and scam podcasts](/blog/best-fraud-scam-podcasts).

## If you want history with weight

### Killers of the Flower Moon — David Grann

Grann's book covers the murders of members of the Osage Nation in 1920s Oklahoma, and the early FBI investigation that followed. It is narrative history rather than a podcast-style case file, but it has the same investigative shape: a pattern nobody wants to see, then a slow reckoning.

If you are new to the audiobook format, this is a good place to try it, because the story moves in order and the human cost is front and centre. We have not paired it with a podcast because we do not have one on the site that covers the same ground, and we would rather say that than force a match.

## If you want British cases

### People Who Eat Darkness — Richard Lloyd Parry

Lloyd Parry was The Times's Tokyo correspondent when Lucie Blackman, a young British woman working in Tokyo, disappeared in 2000. His book follows the investigation and the trials that followed. It is patient, humane and long, and it asks a good deal of the listener.

<AffiliateProduct slug="people-who-eat-darkness-0e5-audiobook" />

### Stakeknife: Britain's Secret Agents in Ireland — Martin Ingram and Greg Harkin

The book concerns the alleged agent known as Stakeknife, said to have been an informer inside the IRA run by British intelligence. The allegations were denied by the man named, and the question of what the state knew and allowed is the heart of the subject. **Pair it with** the BBC's [Stakeknife podcast](/podcasts/stakeknife), a 14-episode series we rate a **Must listen**, with a binge factor of 8. It is unusually close to the book's subject and a good example of what the [best BBC true crime podcasts](/blog/best-bbc-true-crime-podcasts) can do.
