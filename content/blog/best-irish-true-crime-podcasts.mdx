The best Irish true crime podcasts are **[West Cork](/podcasts/west-cork)**, **[Where Is George Gibney?](/podcasts/where-is-george-gibney)** and **[The Nobody Zone](/podcasts/the-nobody-zone)**, three finished documentary series about Irish cases, made by or with Irish journalists and broadcasters, plus RTÉ's **[Unsolved](/podcasts/unsolved-rte)** and the Sunday World's **[Crime World](/podcasts/crime-world-nicola-tallant)** if you want something new every week. They earn their places because they know the places, families and courts they are talking about.

I've split this guide into finished series, weekly shows, newer releases and Northern Ireland. Where a show has an editorial verdict on its profile here, I've given it; those verdicts are our opinion, and [how we review](/how-we-review) explains them. Episode counts and dates come from Apple Podcasts and the broadcasters' own pages, checked on 19 November 2026. Several of these cases involve living families, including children, so I've kept the detail to what you need to choose.

## The landmark series: start here

### West Cork

Sam Bungey and Jennifer Forde's 13-episode documentary is about the murder of the French film producer Sophie Toscan du Plantier, whose body was found near her holiday home outside Schull on 23 December 1996. It was released as an Audible Original in 2018 and became a free podcast through Acast in March 2021. The free feed now has 15 items: the 13 episodes, a May 2021 feature episode on the French trial, and a short trailer.

What makes it last, for me, is that it is as much about a community as a crime. The man gardaí suspected, the English journalist Ian Bailey, was never charged in Ireland. A Paris court convicted him in his absence in 2019; he always denied involvement, and Ireland's High Court blocked his extradition. He died in January 2024, after the series was made.

**Our verdict:** Must listen. **Where to start:** Episode 1, "Blow-ins", and listen in order. **Caveat:** it predates Bailey's death, and it moves slowly by design.

### Where Is George Gibney?

Reporter Mark Horgan and producer Ciarán Cassidy of Second Captains made this ten-part series for BBC Sounds in 2020, after a two-year investigation. Gibney was a celebrated Irish Olympic swimming coach who faced 27 child sexual abuse charges in 1993 but did not stand trial on them, following a Supreme Court ruling. RTÉ reports that the series led more people to come forward. He was arrested in Florida in 2025, convicted in July 2026 of 39 counts of indecent assault and one count of attempted rape, and jailed for 15 and a half years in October 2026. The series follows his life afterwards in the UK and the US and gives much of its time to former swimmers who say he abused them.

I'd put it among the most important pieces of Irish audio journalism, because it keeps the survivors at the centre.

**Our verdict:** Must listen. **Where to start:** Episode 1, "It Happened in the Summer", on [BBC Sounds](https://www.bbc.com/audio/series/p0k383l7). **Caveat:** it was made before his arrest and trial, and the BBC warns that it refers to child sexual abuse and includes interviews some listeners will find upsetting.

### The Nobody Zone

This is a co-production between RTÉ's Documentary On One and Third Ear in Denmark, written and narrated by Tim Hinman, with original research by Rob Mulhern, Ronan Kelly and Liam O'Brien. It tells the story of Kieran Patrick Kelly, a homeless Irishman in London who confessed to multiple murders in 1983. According to RTÉ, a retired police officer said Kelly had confessed to 31 killings, though he was convicted of only two.

The series is open about how hard it is to know what to believe, and that doubt is the point. So is the question of why nobody noticed the people around him.

**Our verdict:** Must listen. **Where to start:** Episode 1, "It Plays On Your Mind" (February 2020), and listen in order through the six-part series and the two follow-up episodes from late 2020. **Caveat:** the feed also carries a bilingual Irish-language version, so check you have the English episodes.

## Ongoing weekly shows

### Crime World

Crime World with Nicola Tallant, the Sunday World's investigations editor, describes itself as "a weekly Irish podcast about criminals, drugs and the sins of the underworld", though new episodes often arrive several times a week. Many episodes are conversations with colleagues such as Niall Donald about that week's courts and gangland news. Alongside those come multi-part series, such as Ghost, about the disappearance of Sandra Collins from Killala, Co Mayo, in 2000.

**Our verdict:** Good if Irish true crime interests you. **Where to start:** a self-contained series such as Ghost, rather than the newest episode. **Caveat:** Apple listed 1,950 episodes, some series are for Crime World subscribers only, and the writing is in the paper's punchy tabloid style.

### Unsolved (RTÉ)

Unsolved is RTÉ's weekly cold-case podcast, presented by the crime journalist Barry Cummins and the 2FM presenter Katja Mia, and it launched in July 2026. RTÉ says Cummins has 30 years' experience in radio, television and print, much of it on missing people. The show describes itself as putting "the victim firmly at the centre of the story", which is the right instinct for cases this raw. New episodes arrive on Wednesdays; this month's cases include Deirdre Jacob.

**Our verdict:** not set yet; it is too new. **Where to start:** the two-part Arlene Arkinson story, released on 29 July 2026. **Caveat:** these are open cases with living families, and some run over several episodes, so you won't always get an ending.

### Lines of Enquiry

A GoLoud Original presented by the former Garda detective John Sweetman, [Lines of Enquiry](/podcasts/lines-of-enquiry) started in November 2025 and takes an Irish or Irish-linked case at a time, looking at how evidence, chance and persistence brought answers, or didn't. A detective's perspective is what sets it apart here.

**Our verdict:** Good if Irish true crime interests you. **Where to start:** the first full episode, "Mary Gough: Death on the Stairs", or a case you already know. **Caveat:** recent episodes run from 40 minutes to over an hour, and the show carries a sponsor.

### Ireland Crimes and Mysteries

[Ireland Crimes and Mysteries](/podcasts/ireland-crimes-and-mysteries) is presented by Nules, a former nurse according to GoLoud, who joined the GoLoud network in October 2025. She covers solved and unsolved cases and older history, and GoLoud describes the show as focused on victims and their families rather than sensation.

**Our verdict:** not set yet. **Where to start:** the earliest episode in the feed, on the disappearance of Jo Jo Dullard. **Caveat:** bonus material sits on Patreon, and the feed mixes case episodes with weekly roundups.

## Newer Irish series (2025–26)

### Forever My Boys

*Content note: this series is about the murder of two children by their father, and it includes references to suicide and violence against children.*

In July 2013, Eoghan Chada, 10, and his brother Ruairi, 5, were murdered by their father, Sanjeev Chada, who pleaded guilty and was given two life sentences in October 2014. In [Forever My Boys](/podcasts/forever-my-boys-kathleen-chada), an Irish Sun series from July 2026, their mother, Kathleen Chada, tells the story herself, with others who were there, over three episodes, plus a bonus in which she talks with Angela Doyle Stuart about sentencing and parole.

**Our verdict:** Good if you want a case told directly by the family, not just about them. **Where to start:** Episode 1, "A Happy Family". **Caveat:** the show warns of explicit content, and the third episode's description says it goes into detail not made public before, so read the notes first. The feed also holds other Irish Sun series; look for the July 2026 episodes.

### This is Robert

Robert Delany, a postman and father, was shot at his home in Tallaght in October 2008 and left with catastrophic brain injuries. [This is Robert](/podcasts/this-is-robert-irish-sun), a 2025 Irish Sun series narrated by Damien Lane, tells his story through his parents, Terry and Noeleen, his daughters and his sister. The show says he was targeted for breaking up a pub fight, and that his family still hope whoever ordered the shooting will be caught.

**Where to start:** Episode 1, "A Life Stolen". **Caveat:** only three episodes, and the family's conversations about Robert's care are very hard going.

### Kinahans Downfall

This Irish Sun feed began in 2023 as The Kinahans, a ten-part history written and produced by UrbanMedia, and returned in 2026 as [Kinahans Downfall](/podcasts/kinahans-downfall). In June 2026 it added two episodes with Noel Kirwan's children, Donna and Kristopher. Kirwan was shot dead outside his home in December 2016. On 8 June 2026, Sean McGovern was jailed for 24 years at the Special Criminal Court after pleading guilty to two counts of directing the activities of a criminal organisation, in connection with that murder and the plot to kill James Gately.

Two points of legal fact. Gerry Hutch, who features in the series, was found not guilty in 2023 of murdering David Byrne at the Regency Hotel. And Daniel Kinahan was extradited to Ireland in August 2026 and charged at the Special Criminal Court with directing the activities of a criminal organisation between 2015 and 2017. He is remanded in custody, and his solicitor has said he will contest the charge. That case has not been tried, he is entitled to the presumption of innocence, and the series' 2026 episodes were released before he was extradited and charged.

**Our verdict:** Good if organised crime journalism interests you. **Where to start:** Season 1, Episode 1, "Silent Partner". **Caveat:** its language is often blunter than a court's, so treat its framing as the paper's, not a finding.

## Northern Ireland

### Assume Nothing: Murder at the Stables

Assume Nothing is a BBC Radio Ulster strand that looks at events "through fresh eyes", one series at a time. [Murder at the Stables](/podcasts/assume-nothing-murder-at-the-stables) is about Katie Simpson, a 21-year-old showjumper whose death in 2020 was first treated as suicide. The horse trainer Jonathan Creswell was accused of her rape and murder, which he denied. He was found dead at home on the second day of his trial in April 2024, so no verdict was reached. The series began in November 2024, with five more episodes in November 2025.

**Our verdict:** Good if you want Northern Irish true crime. **Where to start:** Episode 1, "A Wanted Man". **Caveat:** some scenes are dramatised by actors, and the wider Assume Nothing feed is not all crime; one recent series is about early 20th-century experiments with a medium. For another view of the same case, Crime World Presents: Groomed covers it too.

## How to choose

- **One finished story:** West Cork, then The Nobody Zone.
- **Gangland:** Crime World for the weekly news, Kinahans Downfall for the long view.
- **Cold cases and missing people:** Unsolved, Lines of Enquiry or Ireland Crimes and Mysteries.
- **A family's own account:** Forever My Boys or This is Robert, with care.
- **Ads and paywalls:** West Cork and the RTÉ and BBC series are free; Crime World and Ireland Crimes and Mysteries keep some extras for paying supporters.
