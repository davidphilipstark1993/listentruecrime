The best cult podcasts are not really about strange beliefs. They are about control: how ordinary people are recruited, how a group closes around them, and what it costs to leave. The shows below take that seriously. They rely on former members' accounts, court records and reporting rather than spectacle, and they avoid treating survivors as gullible.

We have split them into two groups: **documentary series** that tell one group's story from start to finish, and **ongoing shows** that cover a different group or theme each episode. The shows with a link have a full review and binge score on our site. The others are well-known series we recommend but have not reviewed yet.

Some of these stories involve sexual abuse, suicide and the deaths of children. We note where a series covers them.

## Documentary series: one group, start to finish

### Heaven's Gate

**The best place to start.** Glynn Washington, who grew up in the Worldwide Church of God and left it at 19, tells the story of the group whose members died in Rancho Santa Fe in 1997. Produced by Stitcher and Pineapple Street Media, the ten episodes talk to former members and the families of those who died. Washington understands why the group appealed to people, and that makes the ending more painful.

**Covers:** mass suicide.

### Uncover: Escaping NXIVM

The first season of CBC's investigative series, released in September 2018, follows Sarah Edmondson, who spent 12 years in NXIVM and ran its Vancouver centre before leaving and speaking to the New York Times. CBC producer Josh Bloch, a childhood friend of Edmondson's, reported the series. It came out before Keith Raniere's trial and two years before the TV documentaries about NXIVM, so it captures the story as it was happening. Later seasons of the series cover other subjects, so start with Season 1.

**Covers:** sexual coercion and branding.

### Two-Faced: John of God

João Teixeira de Faria, known as John of God, was a Brazilian faith healer visited by thousands of people a year, including international celebrities. In 2018, hundreds of women accused him of sexual abuse, and Brazilian courts have since convicted him. Journalist Martina Castro's 2026 series, from Exactly Right and Adonde Media, looks at how a healing reputation protected him. It is also available in Spanish as *Dos Caras: Juan de Dios*.

**Covers:** sexual abuse.

### Devil in the Dorm

In 2010, Larry Ray moved into his daughter's student housing at Sarah Lawrence College and over the following decade controlled and exploited a group of her friends. He was convicted in 2022 of crimes including extortion and sex trafficking. This six-part Law&Crime series, narrated by Elisabeth Rohm, explains how he took control of them. It shows that a cult does not need a compound or a religion: here it was a handful of students and one man.

**Covers:** psychological abuse and sexual exploitation.

### Deceived: The Daybell Story

KSL reporter Nate Eaton, who covered the case from the start, tells the story of Lori Vallow and Chad Daybell, whose extreme end-times beliefs ended in the murders of Vallow's children and Daybell's wife. Both have been convicted. It is less a cult in the usual sense than a small circle of belief, but it shows how quickly that can become dangerous. New episodes were still being released in November 2026.

**Covers:** the deaths of children.

## Ongoing shows: a different group each episode

### Cults

Parcast's long-running series, hosted by Greg Polcyn and Vanessa Richardson, usually spends two episodes on each group, from Jonestown to recent cases. It is scripted, easy to follow and the quickest way to work out which stories you want to hear more about. Because it covers so much, it rarely goes as deep as the documentary series above.

### Trust Me

Hosts Lola Blanc and Meagan Elizabeth interview former members, experts and journalists. It is our pick for understanding how manipulation works in everyday life, not just in famous groups.

### A Little Bit Culty

Hosted by Sarah Edmondson and Anthony "Nippy" Ames, both former NXIVM members. After Escaping NXIVM, this is the natural next step: it covers recovery, and how to recognise coercive control.

### Conspiracy Theories, Cults and Crimes

A [Crime House show](/podcasts/conspiracy-theories-cults-and-crimes) with Vanessa Richardson about belief taken too far: cults, conspiracy movements and the crimes linked to them. Lighter than the documentary series, but well organised.

## How cults work: what the best shows explain

Every strong show on this list arrives at the same answer to the question "how could people fall for this?" People do not join high-control groups because they are stupid or weak. They join because the group offers something real (community, purpose, certainty, a fix for a problem) and the control comes later, step by step, once leaving would mean losing all of it.

That is why former members' voices matter so much here. The best series let survivors explain their own decisions, instead of having a narrator explain them from outside.

If a show leans on the strangeness of a group's beliefs for laughs, or calls its members brainwashed without listening to them, it is telling you about the presenters more than the group.
