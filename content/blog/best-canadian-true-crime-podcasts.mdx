Canada's contribution to true crime podcasting is disproportionate to its population. A country of 38 million has produced two of the most important investigative podcasts ever made — work that stands comparison with the best audio journalism produced anywhere, including the BBC and NPR.

Here's what Canadian true crime is doing that the rest of the world should pay attention to.

## Hunting Warhead: The Most Important Investigative Podcast Ever Made

**Hunting Warhead** is a joint production between CBC and Vice News, investigating the takedown of the dark web's largest child sexual abuse material network.

Journalists Daemon Fairless and embedded Vice reporters spent months inside the actual international law enforcement operation — following investigators from Europol, Interpol, and multiple national police agencies — that ultimately dismantled a network with hundreds of thousands of members.

The result is audio journalism of an extraordinary order. It's harrowing — this is not listening for entertainment — but it's vital. The victims of CSAM networks are real people whose ongoing exploitation is one of the most serious and underreported crimes in the world. Hunting Warhead treats them accordingly.

It won multiple journalism awards, including the Canadian Podcast Award for Best Investigative Podcast. It deserved every one.

**Rating: 9/10** — Not for everyone, but arguably the most important podcast in the genre

## Missing & Murdered: Finding Cleo — Journalism That Changed Policy

**Missing & Murdered: Finding Cleo** is CBC journalist Connie Walker's investigation into the disappearance of Cleo Semaganis Nicotine, a Cree woman taken from her family as a child during Canada's Sixties Scoop — the government policy under which thousands of Indigenous children were forcibly removed from their families and placed with white foster and adoptive parents.

Cleo disappeared as an adult. When Walker began investigating, she found not just a missing person case but evidence of how Canada's child welfare system — including the Sixties Scoop — had systematically destroyed Indigenous families, and how that destruction made Indigenous women and girls more vulnerable to violence and disappearance.

The podcast contributed to changes in how the Canadian government officially recognises and investigates missing Indigenous women and girls. It remains one of the finest pieces of journalism produced in podcast form.

**Rating: 9/10** — Essential

## Crime Beat: Newspaper Journalism in Audio Form

**Crime Beat** — produced by Calgary Herald journalist Nancy Hixt — applies the rigour and ethics of newspaper journalism to Canadian crime stories in audio form.

This isn't narrative podcast production — it sounds more like quality radio journalism than cinematic storytelling. But the research standard is excellent, and it covers Canadian cases that receive virtually no attention from the US-dominated true crime podcast landscape.

**Rating: 6/10** — Reliable and informative

**Canadian True Crime**, hosted by Kristi Lee, covers Canadian cases with good production quality and genuine commitment to victim advocacy. The independent production works against the Casefile model — regular episodes, varied cases, consistent research standard.

## The Trail Went Cold: Robin Warder's Weekly Cold Cases

**The Trail Went Cold** — produced by Robin Warder — is one of the most carefully researched cold case podcasts in the genre. It operates in the shadow of bigger shows and gets a fraction of their audience, but the quality of research consistently exceeds what the audience size would suggest.

Warder covers a new cold case every week, focusing on cases that aren't getting coverage elsewhere. His commitment to accuracy and victim respect is genuine and consistent.

**Rating: 6/10** — Consistently underrated

## The Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls Crisis

No guide to Canadian true crime can avoid engaging with this. Indigenous women and girls in Canada are murdered at more than six times the rate of non-Indigenous women. Hundreds of cases have never been adequately investigated. The National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls — a three-year federal commission — concluded in 2019 that the violence constitutes a genocide.

True crime podcasting has played a meaningful but insufficient role in addressing this. Missing & Murdered: Finding Cleo is the most important podcast on the subject, but Connie Walker's own subsequent work (Somebody's Daughter) continues the investigation.

The issues are structural: a police system that has historically treated missing Indigenous women as lower priority; a child welfare system that has continued in modified form the family separation of the Sixties Scoop; a geographic reality in which many Indigenous communities are in remote areas far from investigative resources.

These cases deserve sustained, serious attention. The podcasting community has begun to provide it. More is needed.

## Dark Poutine: Comedy True Crime, Canadian Style

**Dark Poutine** brings Canadian true crime with dark humour, and does it better than most comedy crime podcasts manage. The hosts genuinely care about the cases they cover, the research is solid, and the comedy comes from the absurdity of how crimes unfold rather than from the suffering of victims.

**Rating: 6/10** — A worthy addition to the Canadian podcast landscape
