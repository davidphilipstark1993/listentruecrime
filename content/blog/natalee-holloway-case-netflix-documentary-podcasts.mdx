**Natalee Holloway was an 18-year-old from Alabama who disappeared on a graduation trip to Aruba on 30 May 2005.** She was last seen leaving a bar with Joran van der Sloot, then 17, and two brothers, Deepak and Satish Kalpoe. Van der Sloot was arrested, released and never charged. Eighteen years later, as part of a US plea deal over an attempt to extort money from her family, he confessed to killing her. Her body has never been found, and no one has been convicted of her death. Netflix's three-part documentary *Taken: Natalee Holloway* was released on 4 November 2026.

If you have just finished the Netflix series, you've seen the story largely through the eyes of Natalee's mother, Beth Holloway. This guide fills in the rest: who Natalee was, what happened in Aruba, how the investigation unfolded, what van der Sloot's confession does and doesn't settle, where the case stands now, and which podcasts are worth your time next.

Facts here come from contemporary reporting by the Associated Press, CBS News, NOS and NL Times, Netflix's own listings, and the court record of the 2023 plea, checked on 25 November 2026. Where an account rests only on van der Sloot's word, we say so.

## Who was Natalee Holloway?

Natalee Ann Holloway was born on 21 October 1986 in Memphis, Tennessee, and grew up in Clinton, Mississippi, before moving with her mother, Beth, and stepfather, George "Jug" Twitty, to Mountain Brook, a suburb of Birmingham, Alabama, in 2000. Her father, Dave Holloway, and her younger brother, Matthew, complete the immediate family.

In May 2005 she graduated with honours from Mountain Brook High School, where she was a member of the National Honor Society and the dance team. She had a place at the University of Alabama that autumn and planned to study pre-med.

Most retellings, including the Netflix series, spend far more time on the man accused of killing her than on Natalee herself. It's worth remembering that what made this case famous was not who she was, but how long it took anyone to answer what happened to her.

## The trip to Aruba

On 26 May 2005, Natalee flew to Aruba with 124 fellow Mountain Brook graduates and seven chaperones for a five-day trip. It was an unofficial celebration, not a school-organised one. Natalee stayed at the Holiday Inn.

On the last night, 29 May, a group went to Carlos'n Charlie's, a bar in the capital, Oranjestad. Natalee had met Joran van der Sloot, a Dutch student at the International School of Aruba, earlier in the trip. At around 1:30am on 30 May, friends saw her leave in a car with van der Sloot and the Kalpoe brothers.

She did not appear for the flight home later that day. Her packed luggage and passport were found in her hotel room.

## The investigation in Aruba

The search for Natalee became one of the largest in Aruba's history, involving police, volunteers and Dutch military support, and later private search teams funded by her family. It found nothing.

The investigation itself was criticised from the start, above all by Natalee's family. Beth Holloway publicly accused Aruban authorities of moving too slowly on the three young men she believed were responsible, and of protecting van der Sloot, whose father worked in the island's justice system. Aruban officials, including deputy police chief Gerold Dompig, defended their work and said they did everything in their power to solve the case. Dompig is interviewed in the Netflix series.

The early arrests set the pattern. Two security guards were held on what turned out to be nothing and released. Van der Sloot and the Kalpoes were arrested, held and released, re-arrested in 2007 and released again. Van der Sloot changed his account of the night several times: that he dropped Natalee at her hotel, that he left her on the beach, and later, in interviews and on de Vries's hidden camera, other versions he then disowned.

The Kalpoe brothers were never charged with any crime connected to Natalee's disappearance and have consistently denied involvement in her death. Van der Sloot's 2023 confession describes him acting alone.

## The 2010 extortion attempt and the murder of Stephany Flores

The turning point came in 2010. Van der Sloot contacted John Q. Kelly, the Holloway family's lawyer, offering to reveal where Natalee's body was in exchange for $250,000, with $25,000 up front. Kelly went to the FBI, which set up a sting with Aruban authorities. Van der Sloot was filmed taking $10,000 in cash, and a further $15,000 was wired to his account in the Netherlands. He said his father had buried Natalee under the foundation of a house. The house hadn't been built in 2005, and he later admitted the story was false.

He was not arrested. Days later he left for Peru. On 30 May 2010, five years to the day after Natalee disappeared, he killed Stephany Flores, a 21-year-old business student, in his hotel room in Lima. He was arrested in Chile, confessed, and in January 2012 was sentenced to 28 years.

That sequence is the emotional core of the Netflix series' final episode, whose title, *I Knew He Would Kill Again*, says plainly where Beth stands. Netflix says the series includes previously unreleased footage from the 2010 FBI operation.

## The 2023 confession: what it does and doesn't settle

In June 2023 van der Sloot was temporarily extradited to the US to face the 2010 extortion charges. On 18 October 2023, in federal court in Birmingham, he pleaded guilty to extortion and wire fraud. As part of the deal he agreed to tell Natalee's family what happened to her, in an interview conducted by his own lawyer, and to take a polygraph. Beth Holloway watched the interview from another room. A transcript was later included in court documents.

According to that transcript, as reported by CBS News and the Associated Press, van der Sloot said that he and Natalee were on a beach on the way back to her hotel; that when she refused his advances she kneed him; that he kicked her in the face and then struck her with a cinder block he found nearby; and that he waded into the sea until the water reached his knees and pushed her body out. Judge Anna Manasco sentenced him to 20 years, concurrent with his Peruvian sentence. Beth told reporters afterwards that she believed it was the truth and that, for her, "it's over".

There are good reasons to treat that account as the most credible one he has given: it was made under a plea agreement, with a polygraph condition, and it is consistent with the crime he committed against Stephany Flores. But it's important to be precise about what it is.

- **It is a confession, not a conviction.** He was convicted of extortion and wire fraud. He has never been charged with, tried for or convicted of Natalee's death.
- **It has not been tested.** No one cross-examined it, and no physical evidence has corroborated it.
- **He has lied many times.** Over 18 years he gave several incompatible accounts, including a false burial site he was paid for.
- **Her remains have not been found.** If his account is true, they are unlikely ever to be.

So the fairest summary is that van der Sloot is the man who says he killed Natalee, that her mother believes him, and that no court has yet ruled on it.

## Natalee's family: two parents, two searches

It is impossible to separate the Natalee Holloway case from her family's campaign, which kept it in the news for nearly two decades.

**Beth Holloway** flew to Aruba within hours of hearing her daughter was missing and identified van der Sloot as a suspect almost immediately. She became one of the most recognisable figures in American news coverage of the case, wrote the book *Loving Natalee* (2007), and later turned her experience into advocacy on safety for young people travelling abroad. She is the centre of the Netflix series.

**Dave Holloway**, Natalee's father, ran his own long search, wrote his own book on the case, and asked a court to declare Natalee legally dead in 2012. His search, with private investigator T.J. Ward, was the subject of Oxygen's 2017 series. Netflix's published list of contributors doesn't include him, and its series is built around Beth's account.

Both parents publicly criticised the Aruban authorities, and the case strained relations between Aruba and parts of the US for years. In Aruba, the media coverage and the family's criticism of the island were themselves controversial, a point the more balanced coverage, including some of the podcasts below, takes seriously.

## Netflix's Taken: Natalee Holloway: what the documentary covers

*Taken: Natalee Holloway* is a three-part Netflix docuseries directed by Dani Sloane (*The Menendez Brothers*) and Matthew Galkin (*One Night in Idaho: The College Murders*), produced by Liz Garbus and Dan Cogan's Story Syndicate. It was released on 4 November 2026 and is available on Netflix in the UK.

| Episode | Title | Runtime (Netflix UK) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Natalee is Missing | 40 min |
| 2 | Tangle with the Devil | 38 min |
| 3 | I Knew He Would Kill Again | 38 min |

Episode one covers the trip and the first frantic days of the search. Episode two follows the investigation's failures and Beth's turn to the FBI. Episode three moves to 2010, the murder of Stephany Flores, and Beth's chance to confront the man she believes killed her daughter.

According to Netflix, contributors include Beth Holloway; Natalee's friends Madison Greer and Claire Fierman, and family friend Sue Remick; the family's lawyer John Q. Kelly; former Aruban deputy police chief Gerold Dompig, speaking about the case for the first time in two decades; Aruban journalist Dilma Arends Geerman and businessman Charles Croes; cameraman Tony Zumbado; and retired FBI agents including Alexandra Montilla and Michael Leverock. Netflix also says it includes footage from the 2010 FBI operation that had never been released before.

Netflix's own content advice flags murder, sexual violence and strong language. In the UK the series is rated 12.

## Is the Natalee Holloway Netflix documentary worth watching?

**Yes, if you are new to the case or want to understand it through Beth Holloway's eyes. Less so if you followed it closely and are hoping for new answers.**

<Callout type="verdict">
*Taken: Natalee Holloway* is short, at under two hours, and its strength is access: Beth Holloway, Natalee's friends, the family's lawyer, the FBI agents behind the 2010 sting, and Aruba's former deputy police chief speaking about the case for the first time in two decades. It is not a reinvestigation, and early reviews, including *The National*'s, have noted that it doesn't fundamentally change what was already known. Watch it for the human story and the previously unreleased 2010 sting footage, then use the podcasts below for the detail and the perspectives it leaves out.
</Callout>

**What it gets right:**

- **It puts the family's experience first.** By building the series around Beth, it shows what nearly two decades of false leads, media attention and a suspect's lies cost the people who loved Natalee, which most retellings treat as background.
- **It gives Aruba a voice.** Including Dompig, an Aruban journalist and an Aruban businessman is a real improvement on the 2005 cable news coverage, which often cast the island as the villain.
- **It ends in the right place.** Making Stephany Flores's murder the turning point of the final episode underlines the most uncomfortable fact of the case: the 2010 sting ended with van der Sloot free to leave, and within weeks he had killed again.

**What to keep in mind:**

- **It is one family member's story.** That's a deliberate choice, but Dave Holloway, who ran his own long search, is not among the contributors Netflix lists.
- **It can't resolve the ending.** A confession without a body or a trial leaves the story legally unfinished, and three short episodes leave little room to explain what that means.
- **The confession is not a verdict.** It may well be the truth, but it hasn't been tested in court. The section above sets out what it does and doesn't establish.

## Where the Natalee Holloway case stands in November 2026

Van der Sloot is in prison in Peru, serving his sentences for the murder of Stephany Flores and for drug trafficking in prison. Under his US plea deal, if Peru releases him before his 20-year US sentence ends, he is to be sent to the US to serve the rest of it. In August 2026 NL Times reported that he had asked to be moved to another prison, saying other inmates were threatening him.

The question of a prosecution for Natalee's death has moved to the Netherlands. In January 2026, Aruban prosecutors reviewed documents from the US Department of Justice and concluded there was not enough evidence to prosecute him for murder, and that manslaughter was time-barred in Aruba. On 16 March 2026, Beth Holloway filed a criminal complaint against van der Sloot in the Netherlands, of which he is a citizen, through Dutch lawyer Annemiek van Spanje. In April, Aruba handed its case file to the Public Prosecution Service in Amsterdam so it could assess the complaint. According to ANP, as reported by NL Times, the offence has not expired under Dutch law. As of 25 November 2026 we have found no report of a decision on charges, and it is unclear whether Peru would allow him to be brought to the Netherlands for a trial.

## Why the Natalee Holloway case still matters

**It changed how missing persons cases are covered.** Natalee's disappearance dominated US cable news in 2005 in a way few cases had. Media critics have long cited it as a textbook example of "missing white woman syndrome": the pattern by which some victims get saturation coverage while others, including many women of colour, get almost none. That critique is about the media, not about Natalee or her family, and both things can be true.

**It shows the limits of cross-border justice.** A crime on Dutch-Caribbean soil, a Dutch suspect, an American victim, an FBI sting and a murder in Peru: the case moved through four legal systems, and none has yet tried anyone for Natalee's death.

**The cost of the 2010 sting.** The most painful fact in the story is that van der Sloot was allowed to leave Aruba after taking the family's money, and killed Stephany Flores weeks later. Her family's loss is part of this case too.

## The bottom line

Natalee Holloway disappeared in Aruba on 30 May 2005. The man last seen with her, Joran van der Sloot, confessed in 2023 to killing her, but he has never been charged with her death, her remains have never been found, and a complaint filed by her mother is now with Dutch prosecutors. Netflix's *Taken: Natalee Holloway* is a moving and well-sourced account of Beth Holloway's 18-year fight, but it is one perspective on a case with many. Watch it, then listen to Morbid for the full arc, Casefile for the investigation, and Beth's own recent interviews for where things stand now.
