A true crime fan is one of the easier people to buy for and one of the easier to get wrong. The easy part is that they have a clear interest. The hard part is that the genre is wide: someone who loves a comedy podcast with friends will not necessarily want a long book about a murder trial, and someone deep into cold cases will not want a novelty mug.

This guide is organised by the kind of listener you are shopping for, not by product. It covers books and audiobooks matched to different habits, and a few things from our own shop for people who would rather wear their obsession. Every book is real and described accurately, and we have not rated any of them.

## For someone new to true crime

### Killers of the Flower Moon — David Grann

If the person is just getting into the genre, this is the book we would give. It covers the murders of members of the Osage Nation in 1920s Oklahoma and the early FBI investigation that followed, it moves in order and it is clear about the human cost. It works well in print and as an audiobook, and we have confirmed an audiobook edition on Amazon UK.

It is a serious book about a serious subject, so it is a better gift for someone who likes history than for someone who wants light listening. For a lighter start, point them to our [best true crime podcasts for beginners](/blog/best-true-crime-podcasts-for-beginners).

## For the person who is obsessed with one case

### I'll Be Gone in the Dark — Michelle McNamara

For a listener who loves a long investigation, this is the most natural pick. It is Michelle McNamara's years-long inquiry into the Golden State Killer, completed after her death and published in 2018. Someone who follows serialised podcasts will recognise the pull, and the case has several good podcasts for afterwards, which we cover in our [Golden State Killer guide](/blog/golden-state-killer-podcasts-and-books).

It describes serious violence, so it is not for everyone. Think about whether the recipient is comfortable with that before you buy.

## For the person who wants to understand how offenders think

### Mindhunter — John E. Douglas and Mark Olshaker

Douglas was an FBI agent who interviewed convicted serial killers in the 1970s and 1980s and helped shape the Bureau's approach to profiling. The book is his account, and it was the basis for the Netflix series of the same name. It makes a good gift for someone who liked the show and wants to know where it came from.

It is a memoir, and criminal profiling has since been questioned by researchers, so it is best read as history rather than science. That is worth mentioning in the card if you suspect they will take it as gospel.

## For the fan who likes the comedy side

### Stay Sexy & Don't Get Murdered — Karen Kilgariff and Georgia Hardstark

This is the book by the hosts of My Favorite Murder, and it is the right gift for a fan of the podcast. We list [My Favorite Murder](/podcasts/my-favorite-murder) as good if niche interests you: it is a warm, funny show aimed at people who find straight true crime too heavy. If the person you are buying for loves that style, a book from the hosts is a better fit than a case file.

If they like that style more generally, our guides to [podcasts like My Favorite Murder](/blog/podcasts-like-my-favorite-murder) and [best comedy true crime podcasts](/blog/best-comedy-true-crime-podcasts) are good follow-ups.

## Choosing well

A few habits make the difference between a gift that lands and one that feels off.

**Match the tone to the listener.** Someone who enjoys a comedy podcast may find a grim case file unwelcome. Someone who listens to serious investigative shows like [Serial](/podcasts/serial) or [Casefile](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime) may find a joke mug a bit thin. If you are not sure, a book on a historical case is a reasonable middle ground.

**Think about what a case might mean to them.** True crime is entertainment for many people and a lived experience for some. If you are not certain that the subject is safe for the recipient, choose something more general, or ask.

**Check how they listen.** If you know they use an audiobook service, an audiobook edition may suit them better than print. We cover the question in [Is Audible worth it for true crime fans?](/blog/is-audible-worth-it-for-true-crime-fans).

**Include a way out.** A gift receipt or a note saying you can swap it means the person is free to choose a different book.
