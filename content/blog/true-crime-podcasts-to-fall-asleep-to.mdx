The best true crime podcasts to fall asleep to are **Dead Sleep: True Crime for Bedtime** and **Crime at Bedtime**, both designed for night-time listening, and **[Criminal](/podcasts/criminal)**, which is here for Phoebe Judge's quiet delivery, not because the stories are always gentle. Calm is subjective, so I've gone by how the shows describe themselves, what their episode lists show, and a couple of comments the hosts have made on the record, rather than pretending I've heard every episode.

Plenty of people use a familiar crime story to wind down, then feel a bit awkward about it. You don't need to. You do need to be choosy, because a lot of true crime is edited to keep you alert.

## Built for bedtime

### Dead Sleep: True Crime for Bedtime

Dead Sleep is the closest thing I can point you to that was actually made for this. It is created and narrated by the journalist Nancy Miller, who hosted the *Love & Death* and *The Staircase* podcasts for Max and the *I'll Be Gone in the Dark* podcast for HBO.

The show's own website calls it "a mindful mystery show made for nighttime listening" and uses the tagline "Gripping, not graphic. Suspenseful yet soothing." It also sets out three promises that matter at bedtime: "No gore. No 911 calls. No loud ads." Apple Podcasts describes it as listener-supported and ad-free, and the website points people to Patreon, where subscribers get monthly bonus episodes.

That is the show speaking for itself, and it is why it sits at the top of a sleep list. The honest caveat is length, and how the standard episodes are produced. Recent free episodes on Apple run longer than a short wind-down. When I checked on 12 November 2026, the latest listed episode, *Love, Hate & Real Estate*, ran 1 hour 1 minute, with other recent free episodes at 55 minutes (19 October) and 1 hour 4 minutes (9 October). Apple also lists a subscriber series, DS Naked, of remastered voice-only versions of existing episodes, and notes that original episodes, with music and sound effects, remain on the main feed. If a sudden piece of music would wake you, that is worth knowing before you press play.

You can listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/dead-sleep-true-crime-for-bedtime/id1587064706) and other podcast apps. The show is still adding episodes in 2026.

### Crime at Bedtime

Crime at Bedtime is written and hosted by the Australian podcaster Jack Laurence, of Mashed Pumpkin Productions, and launched in May 2024. The Apple description says the stories are told "in a calm, relaxed style designed for listening as you drift off to sleep", with "no loud sound effects or over-the-top dramatics".

Laurence built it after listeners used his other shows as a sleep aid. At launch he told RadioToday: "Since launching One Minute Remaining and Wanted, I've had countless people tell me that they fall asleep to my shows every night." RadioToday also reported that the show mixes mindfulness and breathing exercises with crime stories. That was the plan in May 2024, and I haven't confirmed that every current episode still includes those exercises.

This is the shorter option of the two bedtime shows. Recent episodes typically run about 20 to 32 minutes. On 12 November 2026, Apple listed two seasons, 321 episodes and a Clean rating, and the feed was still active, with the most recent episode dated 11 November 2026.

The caveat is about subject matter, not tone. Season 2, which began in February 2026 and is often branded "Mysteries at Bedtime" in the episode notes, leans into unsolved mysteries and strange events, such as the Rendlesham Forest incident, the Dyatlov Pass and UFO sightings. Not every episode is a crime. Season 1 is still running alongside it, with regular crime stories, so the title alone does not tell you which kind of episode you have opened. The free feed also promotes a paid ad-free option, which is a fair clue that the standard episodes include adverts. I cannot, from the public listings, tell you where those adverts sit.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/crime-at-bedtime/id1744360946) and other apps. Episodes are self-contained, so you can start with a case or a mystery that appeals.

## Calm voices from regular shows

### Criminal

**[Criminal](/podcasts/criminal)** is not a bedtime show. It is a regular true crime podcast, hosted by Phoebe Judge and co-created with Lauren Spohrer, and it launched in January 2014. The official site, [thisiscriminal.com](https://thisiscriminal.com), uses the line "stories of people who've done wrong, been wronged, or gotten caught somewhere in the middle." New episodes come out weekly on Fridays, according to the site, and the show is part of the Vox Media Podcast Network. The New York Times named it one of the best podcasts of 2023.

It is on this list because of Judge's delivery. In a December 2023 interview with the 9th Street Journal, she said "the best compliment that I ever got about 'Criminal' was when someone said 'Criminal' is a quiet show." That is a comment about how the show is spoken, not a promise that the stories are mild.

Recent Friday episodes in the feed often run around 35 to 45 minutes, though some are longer. The latest numbered episode on the official site, *Northernmost*, was published on 6 November 2026 and runs about 36 minutes. The feed also carries extra midweek episodes, including interviews.

The caveat is straightforward: this is still true crime, sometimes about murder and other serious cases, and it was not made to send you to sleep. If a case is likely to keep your mind working, leave it for the morning. If you want Judge's voice without the crime, she also hosts *Phoebe Reads a Mystery*, in which she reads a mystery novel a chapter at a time. It has not published new chapters since December 2025, but the back catalogue is still available. That one is not true crime, and I mention it only as a side note.

## What makes a true crime podcast good for sleep

I wouldn't dress this up as science. These are practical things to look for if you want a story to fade into the background rather than jolt you awake:

- Steady narration, without a sudden change in volume or pace
- No abrupt sound effects
- No 911-call audio
- Adverts at the start, if they have to be there, rather than in the middle of a quiet passage
- Episodes of about 20 to 45 minutes, so a single story can finish, or at least find a natural pause
- Self-contained stories, so you are not kept awake by a cliffhanger

Shorter episodes help if you want something that ends before you drop off.
