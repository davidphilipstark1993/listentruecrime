Nineteen years after Madeleine McCann disappeared from a holiday apartment in Praia da Luz, the honest answer to "where is she now" is that nobody investigating the case knows. She remains, officially, a missing person rather than a presumed-dead one — a distinction carefully maintained by Portuguese, British and German authorities alike, and the reason the search has never formally ended, even as it's narrowed dramatically from the hundreds of officers involved in 2007 to a small, part-time team today.

## Missing, Not Confirmed Dead

[Operation Grange's](/blog/operation-grange-explained) senior investigators have said publicly, at various points since 2011, that they treat Madeleine's disappearance as a criminal act by a stranger, without formally committing to whether she's alive or dead. That position isn't wishful thinking so much as an accurate reflection of the evidence: no body has ever been found, despite searches in 2008, 2014, 2020, 2023 and 2025. In the absence of physical remains, missing-person protocol in most jurisdictions keeps a case open on the working assumption that the person could still be found alive, however slim that possibility becomes with time.

## Early Sightings

In the days immediately after Madeleine vanished, Portuguese police pursued several leads that briefly looked significant. CCTV footage from a petrol station near Lagos reportedly showed a child matching Madeleine's description with a woman and two men — a lead that, like so many others in this case, went nowhere conclusive. Weeks later, a witness reported seeing a child matching Madeleine's description getting into a German-registered van outside a restaurant in Alcossebre, Spain, on 28 May 2007, prompting an Interpol alert. It was never conclusively verified at the time, though what's since become known about [Christian Brueckner's](/blog/who-is-christian-brueckner-madeleine-mccann) use of a distinctive camper van in the same period has kept this particular early sighting circulating in case discussion for years.

## The Long Tail of Later Sightings

In the years since, reported sightings have surfaced periodically from across Europe and beyond — Scotland Yard's own review had catalogued more than 8,000 by the time Operation Grange became a full investigation in 2013. The overwhelming majority were well-intentioned misidentifications: children who resembled age-progression images released by police, or blonde, light-eyed girls of roughly the right age spotted by members of the public primed, understandably, to look for her everywhere. None has ever been substantiated.

Age-progression imagery — computer-generated projections of what Madeleine might look like at a given age — has been produced periodically to mark anniversaries and refresh public appeals, built using standard forensic ageing techniques applied to family photographs. These images serve a genuine investigative purpose. They've also, less helpfully, fuelled some of the false-identification cases described below, by giving people a face to compare themselves or others against without any of the underlying certainty a real identification would require.

## Julia Wandelt: The Most High-Profile False Claim

The most sustained and damaging false claim came not from a stranger reporting a sighting, but from a young woman convinced she was Madeleine herself. Julia Wandelt, a Polish woman then in her early twenties, pursued that belief between June 2022 and February 2025, contacting Kate and Gerry McCann and their now-adult twins by phone, email and letter, and travelling from Poland to confront the couple twice outside their Leicestershire home.

<Callout type="warning">
Operation Grange's own DNA testing, taken after Wandelt's arrest in February 2025, conclusively ruled out any match. A jury at Leicester Crown Court acquitted Wandelt and co-defendant Karen Spragg of stalking in November 2025, but found Wandelt guilty of harassment; she was sentenced to six months, already served through time in custody, with a restraining order. Spragg was acquitted of all charges. Full detail is in our [piece on Kate and Gerry McCann's 19-year fight](/blog/kate-and-gerry-mccann-19-year-fight).
</Callout>

## Why False Claims Keep Happening

Cases like Wandelt's aren't unique to the McCann investigation, but Madeleine's case has proven unusually fertile ground for them: extensive age-progression imagery released publicly over the years, an enormous and enduring media footprint, and the increasing availability of consumer DNA and AI tools that can create a false sense of scientific backing for what is, ultimately, a psychological conviction rather than an evidenced identity claim. It's worth being clear-eyed about what these episodes cost the family directly — each false claim, however sincerely held by the person making it, requires Kate and Gerry McCann to revisit their daughter's disappearance in specific, often intrusive detail, with no possibility of the outcome being the one they actually want.

## Unverified Claims Keep Surfacing

Because the case is so widely known, it periodically gets pulled into completely unrelated stories — a document release, a court case, an unrelated investigation — through claims that a witness or informant has mentioned a child resembling Madeleine. In early 2026, one such claim briefly circulated online, tied to an unrelated document release, describing a secondhand, uncorroborated sighting years after Madeleine's disappearance. No investigating authority in Portugal, the UK or Germany has corroborated it, it hasn't been linked to any confirmed location or timeline, and no reputable news organisation has independently verified it. It's included here only to make a point: claims like this circulate regularly, they are not evidence, and treating them as leads worth repeating — rather than noise — is exactly the kind of reporting responsible coverage of this case tries to avoid.

## Why Hope Persists

What keeps this case active, rather than quietly fading the way most missing-person cases eventually do, is a combination of sustained institutional resourcing and a specific, still-developing legal process in Germany. British detectives received a substantial dossier of case files on Christian Brueckner during 2026, and German prosecutors are pushing to overturn his October 2024 acquittal on unrelated charges and secure a retrial — a process that could reshape what evidence becomes available to Operation Grange. Kate and Gerry McCann marked the start of 2026 with a public statement thanking supporters and expressing hope that the year would bring "the breakthrough we long for."

Nineteen years on, that hope remains unresolved, but it isn't baseless: an active investigation, a formally named suspect, and a legal process still working its way through Germany's courts all mean this case is, in the most literal sense, still open.
