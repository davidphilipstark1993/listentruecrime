If you are new to true crime podcasts, the hardest part is not finding a good show. It is knowing which *kind* of show you want. True crime covers everything from Pulitzer-level journalism to two friends chatting about a case over a drink, and starting with the wrong one can put you off the whole genre.

This guide explains the main styles in plain terms and gives one easy starting point for each, plus gentler options if you are nervous about violent content. It is deliberately short. When you know what you like, our ranked list of the [best true crime podcasts](/best-true-crime-podcasts) has 25 more to choose from.

## The types of true crime podcast

| Style | What it sounds like | Commitment | Start with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Documentary series | One case told across a season, like a TV drama | 5–10 hours, listen in order | [Serial](/podcasts/serial) or [Dirty John](/podcasts/dirty-john) |
| Narrated case anthology | A narrator tells a new case each episode | Dip in anywhere | [Casefile](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime) |
| Host-led weekly show | One or two hosts walk you through a case | Dip in anywhere, often under an hour | [Crime Junkie](/podcasts/crime-junkie) |
| Comedy and conversation | Friends talk through cases with jokes and tangents | Long, loose episodes | [My Favorite Murder](/podcasts/my-favorite-murder) |
| Investigative journalism | Reporters dig up new evidence | Usually a season, in order | See our [investigative podcasts guide](/blog/best-investigative-journalism-podcasts) |
| Non-violent true crime | Fraud, scams and deception | Usually a short season | [Sweet Bobby](/podcasts/sweet-bobby) |

If you're unsure, start with a documentary series. It has a beginning and an end, it is usually the best produced, and you will know within two episodes whether true crime is for you.

## If you want one gripping story: Serial or Dirty John

**[Serial](/podcasts/serial)** Season 1 is the show that made true crime podcasts mainstream in 2014. Sarah Koenig re-examines the 1999 murder of Baltimore teenager Hae Min Lee and the conviction of her ex-boyfriend, Adnan Syed. It treats listeners as intelligent adults, never dwells on violence and keeps you asking questions. Start at Episode 1 and listen in order.

**[Dirty John](/podcasts/dirty-john)** is shorter: six episodes from the Los Angeles Times and Wondery about Debra Newell and the man she married, John Meehan, who was not who he said he was. Reporter Christopher Goffard tells it with real tension, and you can finish it in an evening or two.

## If you want a new case every episode: Casefile

**[Casefile](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime)** is narrated by an anonymous Australian host in a calm, measured voice, with no banter and careful research. Cases come from Australia, the UK, the US and elsewhere. Episodes are long, often over an hour, so it suits drives, walks and housework. Because each case stands alone, you can pick any episode whose title catches your eye.

## If you want short, accessible weekly episodes: Crime Junkie

**[Crime Junkie](/podcasts/crime-junkie)** is one of the most popular true crime podcasts in the world. Ashley Flowers tells the story and her co-host Brit Prawat reacts, in episodes that usually run 30 to 45 minutes. It is easy to follow and often focuses on missing people and unsolved cases. It is lighter on depth than a documentary series, but it is an easy weekly habit. For more like it, see [podcasts like Crime Junkie](/blog/podcasts-like-crime-junkie).

## If you want company and laughs: My Favorite Murder

**[My Favorite Murder](/podcasts/my-favorite-murder)** is two comedians, Karen Kilgariff and Georgia Hardstark, each telling the other a case they have read about, with plenty of tangents. It is warm, funny and less carefully researched than the shows above, which is the trade-off. If it appeals, our [comedy true crime guide](/blog/best-comedy-true-crime-podcasts) has more in the same style.

## If you'd rather avoid violence: Sweet Bobby or The Missing Cryptoqueen

**[Sweet Bobby](/podcasts/sweet-bobby)**, from Tortoise Media, is about Kirat Assi, a London radio presenter who was deceived by a fake online relationship for almost a decade. It is a deception story with no murder, and the reveal is extraordinary.

**[The Missing Cryptoqueen](/podcasts/the-missing-cryptoqueen)** follows the BBC's investigation into OneCoin, a cryptocurrency fraud that took billions from investors, and its founder, who vanished in 2017. It is quick, funny in places and has no violence.

## If you want British cases: Murder Mile

**[Murder Mile](/podcasts/murder-mile-uk-true-crime)** covers murders in London, each episode tied to a real street, narrated by Michael Buchanan-Dunne. It's carefully researched and a good contrast to the American shows above. For more, see our [British true crime podcasts guide](/blog/best-british-true-crime-podcasts).

## What to leave until later

- **Shows built on raw recordings.** [Sword and Scale](/podcasts/sword-and-scale) uses real 911 calls and court audio. Many experienced listeners find it too much, and it is not a good first listen. Our list of the [scariest true crime podcasts](/blog/scariest-true-crime-podcasts) is for when you want that intensity.
- **Long-running back catalogues.** You don't need to start at Episode 1 of a show with 500 episodes. Pick a case you've heard of.
- **Thinly researched shows.** If a host is clearly reading out a Wikipedia summary, there is a better show about the same case.

## A simple listening path

1. **Week one:** Serial Season 1 or Dirty John.
2. **Week two:** sample a weekly show (Casefile, Crime Junkie or My Favorite Murder) and see which voice you like.
3. **After that:** follow your interest, whether that's [investigative journalism](/blog/best-investigative-journalism-podcasts), [unsolved mysteries](/blog/best-murder-mystery-podcasts), [cults](/blog/best-cult-podcasts) or [a series to binge in a weekend](/blog/best-true-crime-podcasts-binge-weekend).
