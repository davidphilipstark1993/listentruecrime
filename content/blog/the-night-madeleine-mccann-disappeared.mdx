Madeleine McCann was three years old when she disappeared from apartment 5A at the Ocean Club resort in Praia da Luz, Portugal, on the evening of 3 May 2007. Her parents, Kate and Gerry McCann, had been dining with friends less than a hundred metres away, checking on Madeleine and her twin siblings at intervals through the evening.

Nearly two decades on, no publicly confirmed account explains exactly what happened in the gap between the last time an adult saw Madeleine and the moment Kate McCann found her missing. What follows is a straightforward account of what witnesses said they saw and heard that night, and how those accounts have stood up to almost twenty years of scrutiny.

## Praia da Luz, 3 May 2007

The McCanns were staying at the Ocean Club with a group of seven friends who had travelled from Leicestershire together — a group the press dubbed the "Tapas Seven" (sometimes "Tapas Nine," depending on whether the McCanns themselves are counted). Their ground-floor apartment, 5A, sat a short walk from the complex's tapas restaurant, where the adults ate dinner within sight of the apartment block.

The group's arrangement — leaving the children asleep in unlocked apartments while taking turns to check on them every twenty to thirty minutes — was common practice at resorts like this one at the time. It has since become one of the most examined parenting decisions in recent memory, not because it was unusual, but because it placed several friends walking the same short stretch of road within minutes of each other on the one night it mattered.

## The Checks

The group sat down to dinner at around 8.30pm. Gerry McCann checked on the children at approximately 9.05–9.15pm and found Madeleine and the twins asleep. On his way back to the restaurant he spoke for several minutes with Jeremy Wilkins, another guest walking nearby with his own child. Wilkins later told police he saw no one else on the path during their conversation — no man carrying a child, and no sign of Jane Tanner, whose account would become one of the case's defining details.

Shortly after Gerry's check, Jane Tanner left the table to look in on her own children. Walking the road bordering the apartment, she said she saw a man carrying a child dressed in what she described as pale pyjamas, moving away from the direction of apartment 5A. She mentioned it to a friend that night and reported it to Portuguese police once Madeleine was found missing. For years it sat at the centre of the investigation, prompting an artist's impression and an international appeal for the "man in the tan trousers."

<Callout type="info">
The Tanner sighting did not hold up as evidence of an abduction. In October 2013, the Metropolitan Police's Operation Grange team announced they had identified and ruled out the man Tanner saw: a British holidaymaker carrying his own sleeping daughter back from a nearby creche at almost exactly that time. Senior investigating officer Andy Redwood called it a "revelation moment" — not proof nothing happened, but confirmation that this specific sighting wasn't it.
</Callout>

At around 9.30pm, Matthew Oldfield offered to check the children while looking in on his own next door. He glanced into 5A but didn't enter the room fully, and told the others everything looked normal — a detail scrutinised ever since for what it does and doesn't rule out, since he never actually confirmed Madeleine was in her bed.

## Kate McCann and the Open Window

Kate McCann returned to the apartment at around 10pm. She found the bedroom door open further than she'd left it, pushed it, and a breeze swung it shut. Going in to close it properly, she saw the window and shutter to the room were open — and that Madeleine was not in her bed.

The condition of that window became one of the most contested physical details in the entire case. Portuguese forensic examiners never established definitive evidence of a break-in, which left the investigation without a clean answer to the most basic question it faced: had someone come in from outside, or had the apartment simply been left in a state consistent with the family's own account of the evening?

## Other Witnesses

Several other people came forward with accounts in the days that followed. Martin Smith, a British tourist, told police he saw a man carrying a young, fair-haired girl in pyjama-style clothing near the beach at around 10pm — close to the time Kate discovered Madeleine missing. His account was treated seriously alongside Tanner's, but it was never definitively tied to Madeleine or to any identified suspect, and it remains unresolved rather than disproven.

Robert Murat, a British-Portuguese property developer living locally who offered to help translate for police, was declared an official suspect — an arguido — on 14 May 2007, the first person given that status in the case. He was formally cleared years later and subsequently won substantial libel damages from UK media outlets over their coverage of his arguido status. His brief spell as a named suspect is now understood by investigators to have owed more to local rumour and language-barrier misunderstandings than to substantive evidence — though it shaped the earliest days of press coverage in ways that arguably distorted public understanding of the case for years afterwards.

Alongside these named witnesses, Portuguese police logged hundreds of tips and reported sightings from the surrounding resort in the days after 3 May. The overwhelming majority led nowhere.

## What the Physical Evidence Showed That Night

The immediate physical evidence from apartment 5A was, and remains, limited. There was no confirmed sign of forced entry, and no physical trace pointing definitively to an intruder entering through the window rather than a door simply left unlocked as part of the family's own routine. It would be several months before cadaver dogs examined the apartment and the McCanns' hire car — a separate and heavily contested chapter of the case covered in detail in [our piece on the forensic evidence](/blog/madeleine-mccann-forensic-evidence-explained). What can be said of the night itself is narrower: investigators were left with a timeline built almost entirely from memory rather than forensic certainty, which is exactly why those witness accounts have been picked apart for nearly two decades.

## How These Accounts Have Held Up

Re-reading the record today, a few things stand out. The Tanner sighting, once the case's most famous clue, is now formally regarded by the Metropolitan Police as accounted for by an innocent holidaymaker. Wilkins' account — that he saw no one else on the path during his conversation with Gerry — has never been fully reconciled with Tanner's version of events, though both are treated as honest recollections rather than deliberate inconsistencies. Memory under stress is notoriously imprecise, a point made repeatedly by people who've studied this case closely.

Martin Smith's sighting remains unresolved rather than disproven. What none of the original witnesses could have offered, because it didn't exist yet, is any connection to [Christian Brueckner](/blog/who-is-christian-brueckner-madeleine-mccann), the convicted sex offender German prosecutors named as their suspect in 2020. He has never been charged over Madeleine's disappearance and denies any involvement.

## Why the Details of That Night Still Matter

Any future prosecution — of Brueckner or anyone else — will need to explain how a specific individual moved through Praia da Luz that evening, not just where he happened to be living at the time. That's the practical reason investigators keep returning to the period between 8.30pm and 10pm on 3 May 2007. It's also why the [Portuguese police investigation](/blog/portuguese-police-investigation-madeleine-mccann) that followed, and its early handling of the crime scene, still shapes how confidently anyone can talk about what that night actually looked like.

Nearly two decades later, the account pieced together from a handful of friends, neighbours and holidaymakers on a quiet street in the Algarve remains the starting point every later theory about [what happened to Madeleine](/blog/madeleine-mccann-theories-explained) has to answer to.
