The best true crime podcasts to binge are complete limited series: one story, a fixed number of episodes, and an ending you can reach in a weekend. Every show below fits that description. Each is finished, so you won't be left waiting for the next episode, and each can be heard in roughly 4 to 10 hours.

We picked them for pace and for how well they hold up when heard in one go, not just for the case. Binge scores are our editorial rating of how hard each show is to stop listening to.

Looking for a long-running show to binge instead? Our [binge-worthy podcasts](/category/binge-worthy) page lists every podcast we rate 8/10 or more, including weekly shows with hundreds of episodes.

## The quickest binges

### Dirty John

Six episodes from the Los Angeles Times and Wondery about Debra Newell, a successful interior designer, and John Meehan, the man she married, who was not who he said he was. Reporter Christopher Goffard builds tension steadily and never makes Debra look foolish. It is the best introduction to the limited-series format and you can finish it in an evening.

### Sweet Bobby

Tortoise Media's series about Kirat Assi, a London radio presenter who spent almost a decade in an online relationship with a man who did not exist. It is a deception story with no violence, and each episode adds another layer. A good choice if you want a binge without murder.

### S-Town

From the makers of Serial and This American Life, released all at once in 2017. Brian Reed goes to Woodstock, Alabama, at the invitation of John B. McLemore, who wants him to investigate a murder. The story becomes something else entirely. It is less a crime story than a portrait of a person, and many listeners rate it as the best podcast they have heard.

## A full weekend

### Serial (Season 1)

Twelve episodes on the 1999 murder of Baltimore teenager Hae Min Lee and the conviction of her ex-boyfriend Adnan Syed. Each episode ends on a question that makes you start the next one. The series stops where Sarah Koenig's reporting stopped in 2014, without a verdict. Syed's conviction still stands, though he was released in 2025 when his sentence was reduced. [What happened after Serial](/blog/adnan-syed-after-serial) covers the rest.

### The Dropout

Rebecca Jarvis of ABC News on Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos, the blood-testing start-up that collapsed. It was made before Holmes's criminal trial, using interviews with former employees, patients and investors. Holmes was convicted of fraud in 2022.

### Dr. Death (Season 1)

Laura Beil, a medical journalist, on Christopher Duntsch, a Dallas neurosurgeon who badly injured many patients, some fatally, while hospitals kept letting him operate. It is gripping because it is as much about the system that failed as about the surgeon. Later seasons cover other cases.

### Bear Brook (Season 1)

New Hampshire Public Radio on four victims found in barrels in a state park, and the decades it took to find out who they were. The explanation of genetic genealogy is the clearest of any podcast we know. The subject is dark, including the deaths of children, so pace yourself.

## If you have more time

### Heaven's Gate

Glynn Washington's ten-episode documentary on the UFO religious group whose members died in Rancho Santa Fe in 1997. Longer and heavier than the shows above, but it rewards the time. It is also our first pick in the [best cult podcasts](/blog/best-cult-podcasts).

## How to binge well

- **Start Friday with the shortest show.** Dirty John or Sweet Bobby will tell you how much listening you have in you.
- **Take breaks after the heaviest episodes.** Bear Brook and Heaven's Gate both deal with deaths of children or mass death.
- **Don't start The Teacher's Pet on Sunday night.** You won't want to stop.
