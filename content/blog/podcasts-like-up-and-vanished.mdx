If you loved Up and Vanished, start with Your Own Backyard, then Hell and Gone, then Atlanta Monster from Payne Lindsey himself. All three put one host in a place where a case went cold and let you follow it as it moves. The other six suit different moods, from careful newspaper work to calm Canadian radio.

## What made Up and Vanished work

[Up and Vanished](/podcasts/up-and-vanished) started in August 2016 with Payne Lindsey, then known in Atlanta for directing TV commercials and music videos, looking into the 2005 disappearance of Tara Grinstead, a teacher from Ocilla, Georgia. He wasn't a reporter, and that was the appeal. You heard him learn as he went, and the case moved while he was still releasing episodes. There were arrests in February and March 2017. Nobody has been convicted of Tara's murder. In May 2022 a jury acquitted Ryan Duke of murder and convicted him of concealing her death, and in 2019 Bo Dukes was convicted of concealing a death and related charges.

Later seasons took the same approach to other places: Kristal Reisinger in Colorado, Ashley Loring HeavyRunner on the Blackfeet Nation Indian Reservation in Montana, and Florence Okpealuk and Joseph Balderas in Nome, Alaska.

So what I looked for here is that mix: one person, one place, one cold case, and the sense that the story isn't finished yet.

## Same team, same voice: Atlanta Monster

[Atlanta Monster](/podcasts/atlanta-monster) is the easy first step, because it's Lindsey again, and the feed now comes from iHeartPodcasts and Tenderfoot TV. The feed calls it a series "from the producers of Up and Vanished". It goes back to the Atlanta child murders of 1979 to 1981: the disappearances and murders of more than 25 African American children and young adults.

Wayne Williams was convicted in 1982 of murdering two adults, Nathaniel Cater and Jimmy Ray Payne. Authorities attributed many of the other killings to him, but he was never tried for them, and he has always maintained his innocence. The series sits in that gap.

There are 10 main episodes of around 40 minutes to an hour. Start with "Boogeyman". The caveat: it's marked explicit, and it's a city-wide story from decades ago rather than one small town, so it asks big questions without settling them.

## One person, one case, reported as it happens

### Your Own Backyard

[Your Own Backyard](/podcasts/your-own-backyard) is the closest thing to Up and Vanished season 1 I know. Chris Lambert, from Orcutt in California, got curious after seeing a billboard about Kristin Smart, a 19-year-old Cal Poly student who went missing after an off-campus party over Memorial Day weekend in 1996. He started releasing episodes in September 2019.

Then the case moved. Paul Flores was arrested in April 2021. A jury convicted him of first-degree murder in October 2022, and in March 2023 he was sentenced to 25 years to life. A separate jury acquitted his father, Ruben Flores, of being an accessory. The San Luis Obispo Tribune reported that law enforcement and the Smart family credited Lambert with bringing forward new witnesses and reigniting interest in the case.

Start with "A Face On A Billboard". Episodes often run close to an hour, and the trial coverage is longer still. It's marked explicit, and the last item in the feed is from October 2023, a conversation with the Smart family.

### Hell and Gone

[Hell and Gone](/podcasts/hell-and-gone) follows Catherine Townsend, a journalist and licensed private investigator, for iHeartPodcasts and School of Humans. Season 1 is about Rebekah Gould, a 22-year-old college student who was killed in Izard County, Arkansas, in September 2004. Townsend went to the Arkansas Ozarks to look at a case that had been unsolved for 14 years.

In October 2022 a man pleaded guilty to first-degree murder in Rebekah's death and was sentenced to 40 years. I'm not claiming the podcast caused that, and no source I found says it did.

The show is now a weekly called Hell and Gone Murder Line, with a new case every Thursday. Start with "14 Years Gone" from October 2018. Most episodes run around 30 to 45 minutes. The caveat: it's marked explicit, and the feed mixes old seasons, the weekly show and archive repeats, so pick your episode by title.

### Missing Maura Murray

[Missing Maura Murray](/podcasts/missing-maura-murray) is the citizen detective side of Up and Vanished. Two filmmakers, Lance Reenstierna and Tim Pilleri, started it in July 2015. Maura was a 21-year-old student who drove from Amherst, Massachusetts, to New Hampshire's White Mountains in February 2004 and vanished after a minor single-car accident on Route 112. She has not been seen since.

Start with "1 - Introduction". There are around 150 numbered episodes, many close to an hour, and the newest full one in the feed dates from November 2025. The caveat: it's very long, later episodes drift into other cases, and there is no answer at the end. The case is unsolved.

## A season per case, with the family at the centre

### Someone Knows Something

[Someone Knows Something](/podcasts/someone-knows-something) is CBC's long-running series from the documentary maker David Ridgen. It debuted in March 2016 with Adrien McNaughton, who was five when he vanished on a family fishing trip in eastern Ontario in 1972. Ridgen goes back to investigate, and the first episode is simply called "The family".

Each season takes a different unsolved case, most of them Canadian, and season 10 ran until April 2026. Episodes are mostly around 25 to 50 minutes. Start with "S1 Episode 1: The family". CBC publishes transcripts. It feels like Up and Vanished with the host turned down. The caveat: most of these cases stay unsolved, and the feed now promotes other CBC series too.

### The Lady Vanishes

[The Lady Vanishes](/podcasts/the-lady-vanishes) is about Marion Barter, a teacher and former wife of the Australian soccer great Johnny Warren, who went missing in 1997 after travelling to England. The journalists Alison Sandy and Bryan Seymour worked with Marion's daughter, Sally Leydon, who has been searching for her mother ever since. It started in March 2019 and carried on for years.

In February 2024 the NSW State Coroner found that Marion is deceased and likely died on a date after 15 October 1997, but couldn't determine the place or the cause. The coroner found significant deficiencies and inadequacies in the original police investigation and recommended the case go to, or stay with, the Unsolved Homicide Team. In May 2026 NSW Police raised the reward for information to $1 million.

Start with "Episode 1: Left Behind". Episodes are often close to an hour. The caveat: it's long, and the feed now also carries other shows from LiSTNR.

## More documents, less "me"

### The Teacher's Pet

[The Teacher's Pet](/podcasts/the-teachers-pet), from Hedley Thomas at The Australian, looks at the 1982 disappearance of Lynette Dawson from Sydney's Northern Beaches. It came out in 2018. The ABC later reported that her husband, Chris Dawson, wasn't charged until 2018, after the podcast examined the case.

Dawson was found guilty of her murder in August 2022 after a judge-only trial and sentenced to a maximum of 24 years, with a non-parole period of 18 years. His appeal was dismissed in June 2024, and in June 2025 the High Court refused his final challenge. Lynette's body has never been found.

Start with "Episode 1 - Bayview". Episodes often run well over an hour. The caveat: it's marked explicit, and the feed now carries Thomas's later series and promotions as well.

### Cold

[Cold](/podcasts/cold) comes from Dave Cawley at KSL in Salt Lake City. Season 1, The Susan Powell Case Files, began in November 2018. Susan disappeared from her home in West Valley City, Utah, in December 2009, and she has never been found. Cawley started working on it in 2015, going through public records about the investigation, including police interviews.

Each season takes a different case, and the current one, Deceived, was still releasing when I checked. Start with "To All the Girls I've Loved Before". The caveat: it's hard listening, because Susan's husband later killed the couple's two young sons, Charlie and Braden, and himself. Episodes also often run over an hour.

### Bear Brook

[Bear Brook](/podcasts/bear-brook), from Jason Moon at New Hampshire Public Radio, began in October 2018 with the remains of four people found in barrels in Allenstown, New Hampshire, the first in 1985. In 2017 genetic genealogy identified the likely killer as Terry Peder Rasmussen, who died in prison in 2010 while serving a sentence for another murder. NHPR says it was the first time genetic genealogy had been used to identify a criminal suspect. I've written more about that in my piece on [genetic genealogy and cold cases](/blog/genetic-genealogy-cold-cases).

Moon has said he never imagined the case would start to unravel in the middle of his reporting, which is exactly the Up and Vanished feeling. Season 3, A Private Matter, started on 21 October 2026, and five episodes were out when I checked, the latest on 11 November. Start with "Hide and Seek". The caveat: the feed mixes three seasons and later updates, so check the season number before you press play.

## How to choose

When I checked the feeds on 26 November 2026, this is how they sorted:

- **Shortest binge:** Atlanta Monster, at 10 main episodes.
- **Still releasing:** Hell and Gone Murder Line, Cold and Bear Brook all had new episodes in November 2026.
- **One with a court result:** Your Own Backyard or The Teacher's Pet.
- **One still unsolved:** Missing Maura Murray or Someone Knows Something.
- **Outside the US:** The Lady Vanishes and The Teacher's Pet from Australia, Someone Knows Something from Canada.
- **Payne Lindsey's tone:** Atlanta Monster, then Your Own Backyard.

## An honest note

Shows like these can put real pressure on real people, and neither a podcast's theory nor an arrest is the same as a court finding. Up and Vanished is the example: the man charged with Tara Grinstead's murder, whose name Lindsey says he had never heard before the arrest, was acquitted of it. I've written more about that in [the ethics of true crime podcasting](/blog/ethics-of-true-crime-podcasting).
