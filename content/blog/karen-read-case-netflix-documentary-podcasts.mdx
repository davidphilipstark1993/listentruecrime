**Karen Read is the Massachusetts woman who was tried twice for the death of her boyfriend, Boston police officer John O'Keefe, and acquitted of murder in June 2025.** Prosecutors said she hit him with her SUV outside a house in Canton during a January 2022 snowstorm. She said she was framed by police. A jury convicted her only of drunk driving. Netflix's three-part documentary *The Trials of Karen Read* premiered on 23 November 2026.

If the Netflix series is how you found this case, you've arrived in the middle of a story that has been running for almost five years and is still going in the civil courts. This guide covers what happened, what came out at each trial, what the documentary does and doesn't show, where things stand now, and every podcast worth your time on the case, with links. (If you searched for "Karen Reid", this is the same case: her surname is spelled Read.)

Facts here come from court reporting by [CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/karen-read-trial-timeline-john-okeefe/), [WCVB](https://www.wcvb.com/article/netflix-karen-read-documentary-okeefe-response-nov-23-2026/74511166), [Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/local/karen-read-netflix-series-is-released-story-continues-civil-court/FHMXPTITAVAX5I6DYIGPVQ4PLU/), [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2024/08/27/karen-read-civil-suit-boston-police-boyfriend-death) and the [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/11/09/arts/the-trials-of-karen-read-netflix-docuseries/), last checked on 25 November 2026.

## What happened to John O'Keefe?

On the evening of 28 January 2022, Karen Read, John O'Keefe and a group of friends were drinking in Canton, a town south of Boston, at two bars: C.F. McCarthy's and the Waterfall Bar and Grill. After midnight, Read drove O'Keefe to an after-party at the Fairview Road home of Brian Albert, a fellow Boston police officer.

What happened next is the whole case. O'Keefe did not come home. Early the next morning, Read, frantic, went looking for him with two friends, one of them Jennifer McCabe. They found him in the snow on Albert's front lawn. He was pronounced dead in hospital. The autopsy gave the cause of death as blunt impact injuries to the head and hypothermia.

On 2 February 2022, Read pleaded not guilty in Stoughton District Court to manslaughter, motor vehicle homicide and leaving the scene. In June 2022 a Norfolk County grand jury indicted her on charges of second-degree murder, manslaughter while operating under the influence, and leaving the scene of a collision causing death.

## The prosecution's case

Prosecutors said Read, drunk and angry after an argument, dropped O'Keefe at the house, then reversed her Lexus SUV into him at speed and drove away, leaving him in the snow. The main evidence they relied on was:

- **The taillight.** Read's right rear taillight was broken, and pieces of red plastic were found at the scene.
- **"I hit him."** Several witnesses, including a first responder, testified that Read said things like "I hit him" or "Did I hit him?" that morning.
- **Her blood alcohol.** Tests hours later put her well over the legal limit.
- **The relationship.** Voicemails and texts showed the relationship was under strain that night.

## The defence's case: "a cover-up"

Read's lawyers, Alan Jackson and David Yannetti, argued that O'Keefe went into the house, was beaten there, and was then left outside, and that the people present and the investigators protected one another. They pointed to:

- **The injuries.** The defence said scratches on O'Keefe's arm looked more like a dog bite or attack than a car strike. The Alberts had a dog at the time.
- **The federal investigation.** Federal investigators looking into how the case was handled hired accident reconstruction experts from the firm ARCCA. At trial, the ARCCA experts testified for the defence, and one, biomechanical engineer Andrew Rentschler, said that in his opinion O'Keefe was not hit by a vehicle. Prosecution experts disagreed. The federal inquiry ended without any charges.
- **The investigation.** Investigators never searched inside the house, and evidence was collected in red cups and a grocery bag.
- **Michael Proctor.** The lead investigator, State Police trooper Michael Proctor, had personal ties to people at the house, and texts he sent about Read were crude and insulting. He was questioned about them on the stand.

The people who were at 34 Fairview Road have always denied any involvement in O'Keefe's death. None of them has been charged with any crime connected to it, and in 2026 four witnesses sued Read for defamation over the cover-up claims.

## The first trial and the mistrial (2024)

The first trial began in Dedham in April 2024 before Judge Beverly Cannone. It ran for two months and turned into a national spectacle, with crowds of "Free Karen Read" supporters in pink outside the courthouse.

Proctor's texts were widely seen as a serious blow to the prosecution. On 1 July 2024 the jury said it was deeply divided and Judge Cannone declared a mistrial. Proctor was relieved of duty that day.

Afterwards, according to filings by Read's lawyers, several jurors said they had in fact agreed that Read was not guilty of murder and leaving the scene, and were split only on manslaughter. Her lawyers argued that retrying her on those charges would be double jeopardy. The courts disagreed, including Massachusetts' Supreme Judicial Court on 11 February 2025, so she was retried on everything.

## The retrial and acquittal (2025)

The retrial began with jury selection on 1 April 2025. The Commonwealth brought in special prosecutor Hank Brennan. Proctor had been fired from the State Police in March 2025. The ARCCA experts testified again for the defence.

On 18 June 2025, on the fourth day of deliberations, [the jury found Read not guilty](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/karen-read-trial-timeline-john-okeefe/) of second-degree murder, manslaughter while OUI and leaving the scene. It found her guilty of operating under the influence of alcohol. Judge Cannone sentenced her to a year of probation.

Two other threads ran alongside the trials:

- **Aidan Kearney**, a blogger known as "Turtleboy", championed the cover-up theory and was charged in October 2023 with intimidating witnesses in the case. WCVB reported in November 2026 that his trial on those charges was scheduled for February 2027.
- **John O'Keefe's family**, who have said consistently that they believe Read is responsible for his death and have sued her.

## Netflix's The Trials of Karen Read: what the documentary covers

[*The Trials of Karen Read*](https://www.netflix.com/title/81777231) is a three-episode Netflix docuseries directed by Danielle Johnson Cutts and produced by Sandpaper Films. It premiered on 23 November 2026 and covers O'Keefe's death and both criminal trials, asking, in Netflix's words, whether it was "an accident, a deadly fight or an elaborate cover-up". It is rated TV-MA and runs to about two and a half hours:

1. **"A Broken Taillight"** (47 minutes)
2. **"How Long To Die In The Cold?"** (47 minutes)
3. **"The Evidence"** (51 minutes)

The important thing to know going in is who is and isn't in it.

- **Karen Read is not interviewed.** Producers said Read and her lawyers declined to take part. Her voice comes from earlier media interviews, chiefly a 2023 interview with journalist Gretchen Voss that had previously been released only in snippets, [according to CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/karen-read-trial-documentary-netflix/).
- **The O'Keefe family is.** John's brother Paul O'Keefe appears throughout, as does his niece Kayley Furbush, who was 14 when he died and lived with him. "For people to understand this wasn't just some crazy story," she says.
- **So are key witnesses.** Jennifer McCabe, Brian Albert and Brian Higgins, who were at the house that night, speak at length, as do Colin Albert and Chris Albert. So does former trooper Michael Proctor, who says, "I feel like I let down the O'Keefe family." The second episode concentrates on how Aidan Kearney's coverage affected the witnesses, who describe fearing for their safety.
- **Two retrial jurors** give new interviews, [WCVB reported](https://www.wcvb.com/article/netflix-karen-read-documentary-okeefe-response-nov-23-2026/74511166), including Janet Jimenez, who says, "I really did think that most likely she did it."

### Is The Trials of Karen Read worth watching?

Yes, if you want the side of this story that much of the earlier coverage left out. The coverage that made the case famous was often sympathetic to Read. This series is built around the O'Keefe family, the witnesses and the jurors, and its most valuable material is hearing them at length, along with home videos that make John O'Keefe more than a name in a trial. Paul O'Keefe told WCVB he was "satisfied with the final result". Some of Read's supporters have criticised it as one-sided.

What it isn't is a neutral reference. With Read and her lawyers declining to take part, the defence's case comes mostly through courtroom footage and her earlier interviews, and three episodes can't go through the forensic arguments in the detail the trials did. Watch it alongside something that gives Read's side in her own words: Investigation Discovery's *A Body in the Snow* (below), or Rotten Mango's interview with her after the acquittal.

## Every podcast about the Karen Read case

The Karen Read case was, more than most, a podcast and livestream story: much of the cover-up theory spread through independent shows and YouTube channels before mainstream outlets took it seriously. Below are the podcasts that cover it best, starting with the shows dedicated to the case and then the best single episodes from big true crime podcasts. Every link was checked in November 2026.

### Which Karen Read podcast should you start with?

If you're new to the case, this order works well. Each show is described in full below.

1. **Get the overview:** Crime Junkie's INFAMOUS episode, a single-episode introduction.
2. **Get the whole story:** *KAREN: THE RETRIAL*, the narrative series that runs from O'Keefe's death through the retrial.
3. **Hear Karen Read's side:** the Rotten Mango interview, then the Kelly Ripa episode with Read and Alan Jackson. This is the perspective the Netflix series doesn't have.
4. **Hear the evidence for yourself:** *Canton Confidential* for daily highlights from the retrial, or *Full Trial Audio* for the testimony unedited.
5. **Step back:** Crimes of the Centuries for a measured look after the verdict, and The Murder Sheet's conversation with The Prosecutors for a lawyer's view.

### Podcasts dedicated to Karen Read

**KAREN: THE RETRIAL (Law&Crime / Wondery)**\
*Best for: the full story, told as a narrative series*\
The best place to start if you want the whole story told properly. Law&Crime's original series, *Karen*, covered the death and the first trial; the same feed continued with an eight-part follow-up hosted by Emmy-winning investigative reporter Kristin Thorne, which launched in April 2025 and goes through the corruption allegations, the mistrial and the retrial. It is the source material for the Elizabeth Banks series.\

*Best for: following the retrial day by day*\
A daily show from Boston's NBC station that ran through the retrial, with highlights from each day in court and expert analysis. Good if you want to follow the testimony in order without sitting through the raw feed. The feed has since been renamed *Commonwealth Confidential* and moved on to other Massachusetts cases, but the Karen Read episodes are still there.\

## Where is Karen Read now? The case in November 2026

Read is free and lives in Massachusetts. Her criminal case is over, but several civil cases are not.

- **The O'Keefe family's wrongful death suit.** John O'Keefe's family [is suing Read and the two Canton bars](https://www.wbur.org/news/2024/08/27/karen-read-civil-suit-boston-police-boyfriend-death) that served her that night, the Waterfall Bar and Grill and C.F. McCarthy's, in Plymouth Superior Court. A civil case only has to be proved on the balance of probabilities (a "preponderance of the evidence" in US terms), a lower bar than the criminal standard of beyond reasonable doubt. At [a hearing on 24 November 2026](https://www.wcvb.com/article/karen-read-civil-lawsuit-hearings-nov-24/74518701), the bars asked the court to throw out the claims against them, arguing their staff saw no signs she was intoxicated. The judge took it under advisement. The case was reported to be heading for trial in April 2027.
- **Read's own lawsuits.** Read is suing the Massachusetts State Police and the Town of Canton, alleging a negligent investigation and a conspiracy to point it at her. A judge recently dismissed the conspiracy claim against Canton, and a proposed $100,000 settlement involving the State Police was before the court on 24 November with no ruling yet.
- **The defamation suit.** [In April 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/karen-read-trial-timeline-john-okeefe/), Jennifer McCabe, Brian Albert, Colin Albert and Brian Higgins sued Read and Aidan Kearney for defamation over the claims that they were involved in O'Keefe's death.
- **Michael Proctor.** Fired by the State Police in March 2025, he dropped his appeal for reinstatement in October 2025.
- **Aidan Kearney.** His witness-intimidation case was reported to be scheduled for trial in February 2027.

## Why the Karen Read case became so huge

Plenty of cases have a disputed death and a messy investigation. A few things made this one different.

**It was police on both sides.** The victim was a Boston officer, the house belonged to another, and the investigators were State Police, so the defence's claim was not just that Read was innocent, but that law enforcement had closed ranks.

**The investigator's texts.** Michael Proctor's messages about Read, read aloud in court, convinced many people who had no view on the evidence that the investigation could not be trusted, and they ended his career.

**It was fought online.** Kearney's blog and livestreams, daily YouTube coverage and podcasts built a movement around Read long before the first trial. That's also why the case is so divisive: the witnesses say they were harassed for years over a theory no court has accepted, and they are suing over it. (For more on where true crime coverage can harm the people involved, see our piece on [the ethics of true crime podcasting](/blog/ethics-of-true-crime-podcasting).)

**The verdict didn't settle it.** An acquittal means the jury was not convinced beyond reasonable doubt that Read killed O'Keefe. It is not a finding that someone else did, and no one has been charged. For the O'Keefe family, the question of how John died is still unanswered, which is why the civil case matters to them.

## The bottom line

Karen Read was acquitted of killing John O'Keefe in June 2025, after two trials that exposed serious problems with the investigation, including the conduct of its lead investigator. The Netflix documentary *The Trials of Karen Read* tells the story mostly from the side of the O'Keefe family, the witnesses at the house and the jurors, and Read declined to take part. To get the full picture, watch it, then listen to Read's own account and the trial coverage in the podcasts above, and keep an eye on the civil courts, where the next chapter is being written.
