A murder mystery podcast, in the true crime sense, is a documentary series built around a question that has not been fully answered. Who killed her? Who was she? Was the right person convicted? The nine shows below are all real cases, reported by journalists and documentary makers, and in every one that question is still open or still argued about.

That is what separates this list from our other guides. If you want series where the reporting solved a case or freed someone, see the [best investigative podcasts](/blog/best-investigative-journalism-podcasts). If you just want the strongest true crime overall, see our [best true crime podcasts](/best-true-crime-podcasts).

## Real murder mysteries, fictional whodunits and true crime documentaries

The phrase "murder mystery podcast" covers three different kinds of show, and it helps to know which one you want.

| Type | What it is | Example | On this list? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real-life murder mystery | A documentary about a real killing where a key question is unresolved | West Cork | Yes |

| Fictional murder mystery | Scripted audio drama with actors and an invented plot | Agatha Christie-style whodunits | No, we only review true stories |

## Serial (Season 1)

The series that defined the real-life murder mystery podcast. Sarah Koenig re-examines the 1999 murder of Baltimore teenager Hae Min Lee and the conviction of her ex-boyfriend, Adnan Syed, going through the phone records, the timeline and the testimony of the state's key witness. It never settles whether Syed did it, and that unresolved feeling is why it still works. Syed was released in 2025 after his sentence was reduced, but his conviction stands. Our article on [Adnan Syed after Serial](/blog/adnan-syed-after-serial) explains what happened.

## West Cork

A 13-episode Audible documentary by Sam Bungey and Jennifer Forde about the December 1996 killing of French film producer Sophie Toscan du Plantier at her holiday home near Schull. Ian Bailey, an English journalist living nearby, was the prime suspect for decades but was never charged in Ireland. A French court convicted him in his absence in 2019, Ireland refused to extradite him, and he died in 2024. No one has been convicted in Ireland. The series lets you hear Bailey himself, which makes it unusually unsettling.

## Death in Ice Valley

In November 1970, a woman's burned body was found in a valley near Bergen, Norway. She had used several false identities, and her name is still unknown. This 2018 co-production between the BBC World Service and Norway's NRK reopens the case with new forensic testing and asks listeners for help. It is the purest mystery on this list: the question is not only who killed her, but who she was.

## Black Hands

Five members of the Bain family were shot dead in their Dunedin home in 1994. David Bain, the surviving son, was convicted, then acquitted at a retrial in 2009. The defence said his father, Robin, killed the family and then himself. New Zealand journalist Martin van Beynen, who reported on the 2009 retrial, goes through the evidence for both versions. It is careful and fair, and you can finish it in a weekend.

## The Coldest Case in Laramie

New York Times reporter Kim Barker returns to the town where she grew up to look at the 1985 killing of Shelli Wiley, a University of Wyoming student. It comes from Serial Productions and is as much about memory, and how a town tells itself a story, as about the crime. No one has been convicted.

## Cold

Susan Powell disappeared from her home in West Valley City, Utah, in December 2009. Her husband, Josh, was the main person of interest but was never charged. In 2012 he killed himself and their two young sons. Dave Cawley of KSL Podcasts spent years on the case and uses Susan's own journals and recordings. Susan has never been found. Be aware that the series deals with the deaths of children.

## Up and Vanished (Season 1)

Payne Lindsey started this series as a first-time podcaster looking into the 2005 disappearance of Tara Grinstead, a teacher in Ocilla, Georgia. Two men were arrested in 2017, less than a year after the series began. At trial in 2023, Ryan Duke was acquitted of murder but convicted of concealing her death. Season 1 is rough around the edges, but it is a fascinating record of a case changing while it was being reported.

## Someone Knows Something

CBC documentary maker David Ridgen works with families to reinvestigate unsolved Canadian cases, one per season. Season 1 is about Adrien McNaughton, a five-year-old who disappeared on a fishing trip in 1972. Because each season stands alone, it is the easiest show here to sample: pick the season that sounds most like your kind of question.

## Bear Brook

Four bodies were found in barrels in a New Hampshire state park, two in 1985 and two in 2000, and for decades no one knew who they were. Jason Moon of NHPR follows the genetic genealogists who identified them and the man responsible. The killer is identified, but the victims' identities are the mystery, and the series shows how they were recovered.

## How to choose

- **A real whodunit with a suspect but no resolution:** West Cork.
- **A victim no one can name:** Death in Ice Valley.
- **Two versions of the same night:** Black Hands or Serial.
- **A case still changing:** Up and Vanished.
- **Something you can dip into:** Someone Knows Something.
