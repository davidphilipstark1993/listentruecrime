The best short true crime podcasts are **[Forensic Files](/podcasts/forensic-files)** for a complete case in about 20 minutes, **10 Minute Murder** if you only have a quarter of an hour, and **[Crime at Bedtime](/podcasts/crime-at-bedtime)** or **[America's Most Infamous Crimes with Katie Ring](/podcasts/americas-most-infamous-crimes-with-katie-ring)** for a commute of 25 to 30 minutes.

Episode length is the whole point of this guide, so I haven't guessed it. On 23 November 2026 I checked the episode durations in each show's public podcast feed and took the typical (median) length of its most recent full episodes, leaving out trailers and bonus clips. Where a show varies a lot, I've said so. Shows change, so treat these as a good guide rather than a promise about every episode.

## Under 15 minutes: the quickest true crime podcasts

### 10 Minute Murder

The name is close to accurate. Across its 30 most recent episodes, 10 Minute Murder typically ran about 13 minutes, and almost all of them came in between 11 and 14. Each episode takes one case, from well-known ones such as Joran van der Sloot and Matthew Shepard to lesser-known American murders, and the feed has more than 700 episodes, with new ones still coming out in November 2026.

The caveat is depth. Thirteen minutes is enough for the outline of a case, not the investigation in detail. It is the show I'd pick for a coffee break, not for a case you already know well.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/10-minute-murder-bingeable-true-crime-stories/id1520131270) or in any podcast app. (We don't have a full profile of this show yet.)

### Today in True Crime

Today in True Crime, hosted by Vanessa Richardson for Parcast, ties each episode to a date: what happened on this day in crime history. Episodes in the public feed run about 9 to 16 minutes.

The catch is that the public feed has not added a new episode since 31 August 2022, so treat it as an archive. That still leaves more than 1,000 short episodes, which is plenty if you want a daily ten-minute habit.

## 15 to 25 minutes: one case per episode

### Forensic Files

If you want a short true crime podcast that is consistent every single time, this is it. Forensic Files is the audio of the long-running TV series narrated by Peter Thomas, and recent episodes in its podcast feed run about 20 minutes, almost to the second. Every episode is a standalone case, solved (or broken open) by forensic science, so you can start anywhere and finish before you arrive.

The caveat is age. These are episodes of a TV show, so many of the cases are decades old and the forensic methods are of their time. You won't get updates on what happened next.

### Bite-Sized Crime

Bite-Sized Crime, hosted by Joy Scaglione, focuses on lesser-known cases rather than the famous names, and each recent episode is named after a single victim. The typical episode runs about 17 minutes, and nearly every episode in the feed is under half an hour.

The latest post in the feed (9 November 2026) says new episodes return on 4 January, so the show is on a break, but there are close to 200 episodes to work through.

### True Crime Recaps

True Crime Recaps covers widely discussed cases, often the ones behind recent documentaries and headlines, in a single episode each. Across the whole feed, the typical episode runs about 16 minutes, and nine in ten are under half an hour.

Recent episodes have crept up a bit, mostly 15 to 25 minutes, and the occasional multi-case episode can run over an hour (one in November 2026 ran 75 minutes). Check the length before you press play if you're short on time.

### Criminally Listed

Criminally Listed uses a countdown format: "5 Haunting Unsolved Kidnappings", "3 Unsolved Camping Mysteries" and so on. Recent episodes are typically about 23 minutes, but the range is wide, from about 12 minutes to over 40, with case-update episodes at the long end.

The list format is why it's here. Each case within an episode is short, so you can dip in at the gym or on a walk and not lose the thread if you're distracted for a minute.

## 25 to 30 minutes: built for a commute

### Crime at Bedtime

Crime at Bedtime, written and hosted by Australian podcaster Jack Laurence, is designed to be calm: one self-contained case per episode, told slowly, without loud sound design. Recent episodes typically run 24 to 31 minutes, and new ones come out several times a week.

It was made for falling asleep to, but the same qualities make it an easy commute listen if you don't want a jumpy, music-heavy show. Some episodes are unexplained mysteries rather than crimes, and some are re-runs labelled "From the Vault". There's more on it in our guide to [true crime podcasts to fall asleep to](/blog/true-crime-podcasts-to-fall-asleep-to).

### America's Most Infamous Crimes with Katie Ring

This Crime House show covers big American cases, such as the Waco siege and the Murdaugh murders, in short multi-part arcs. Recent episodes typically run about 26 minutes (most between 20 and 36), and the feed is still releasing new ones in November 2026.

It is not self-contained in the way Forensic Files is: a case is often split into two or three parts, so it suits a week of commutes better than a one-off listen. The feed also carries news-style "HEADLINE" episodes and occasional extras, which can run to about 45 minutes.

## Short single-case series

Not every short listen has to be a standalone case. These are complete series about a single case, with short episodes, so you can finish the whole story in a week of commutes.

### Ada (Casefile Presents)

From the makers of [Casefile](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime), Ada follows the identification of a woman found at a California vineyard in 2011 who stayed a Jane Doe for more than a decade. All seven episodes were released on 26 October 2026. Six of them run about 16 to 23 minutes, and the final episode runs about 46 minutes, for roughly 2 hours 49 minutes in total.

It is a good way to try the Casefile team's research without the main show's long episodes.

### Sarah Payne: Unforgotten (BBC Shadow World)

A six-part BBC series on the disappearance of eight-year-old Sarah Payne in 2000 and what followed, including the campaign for Sarah's Law. The first episodes on the Shadow World feed run about 28 to 29 minutes. On the feed, episodes are coming out weekly from 11 November 2026, and all six are on BBC Sounds as a boxset.

## Not as short as you might think

Two shows that are often recommended for quick listening are, on current evidence, long-form.

**[Crime Junkie](/podcasts/crime-junkie)** is tightly edited, and that's why it gets mentioned. But across the 30 most recent full episodes in its feed, the typical episode ran about 54 minutes. Older episodes were shorter, and a few recent ones still come in around half an hour, but it's no longer a reliable short listen. It suits a long drive.

**[Casefile](/podcasts/casefile-true-crime)** is one of the best-researched shows in the genre, but its recent episodes typically run well over an hour. If you want Casefile-quality research in a shorter format, try Ada (above).

If either is your favourite, we have lists of [podcasts like Crime Junkie](/blog/podcasts-like-crime-junkie) and [podcasts like Casefile](/blog/podcasts-like-casefile).

## Which short true crime podcast suits your listening time?

- **Under 15 minutes (a coffee break):** 10 Minute Murder, or the Today in True Crime archive.
- **About 20 minutes (a short commute or a lunch break):** Forensic Files or Bite-Sized Crime.
- **The gym or a walk:** Criminally Listed. The list format means a missed minute doesn't matter.
- **About 30 minutes (a typical commute):** Crime at Bedtime for a standalone case; Katie Ring if you're happy to follow a case over a few days.
- **A week of commutes:** Ada or Sarah Payne: Unforgotten, one episode per journey.

<Callout type="tip">
Self-contained episodes are the safest choice for a short journey: one case, start to finish, no cliffhanger. Forensic Files, Bite-Sized Crime, 10 Minute Murder and Crime at Bedtime all work that way. Multi-part shows like Katie Ring's are better when you can keep going the next day.
</Callout>

## Why short true crime is harder than it looks

Most true crime podcasters start long and learn to edit shorter. With a compelling case, the instinct is to include everything: every detail, every timeline entry, every witness statement.

A short format forces the question of what actually matters. What does a listener need to understand this case, and what can go without losing the story? The shows that manage it feel complete. The ones that don't feel like they just stopped early.
