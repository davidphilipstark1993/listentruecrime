**The podcasts most like Serial are [In the Dark](/podcasts/in-the-dark), [Your Own Backyard](/podcasts/your-own-backyard), [Bone Valley](/podcasts/bone-valley) and [West Cork](/podcasts/west-cork).** Like Serial Season 1, each one follows a single case across a whole season. A reporter tells it in the first person, works from primary sources, and the outcome is uncertain. Start with In the Dark Season 2 if you want rigour. Start with Your Own Backyard if you want Season 1's sense of someone obsessively trying to work out what happened.

Below are 12 shows we think genuinely hold up for Serial listeners. Each one notes what it investigates, how it's told and whether it's finished. Podcasts that cover a new case every episode are left out, because they're not what Serial fans are usually looking for.

## What makes a podcast "like Serial"?

"Investigative true crime" covers a lot of ground, so here's what we were looking for. Serial Season 1 had four qualities that most of its imitators only partly match:

- **One case, told across a season.** Not a different murder every week.
- **A narrator who doesn't know the ending.** Sarah Koenig's uncertainty was the engine of the show.
- **Reporting, not recap.** The show relied on interviews, court records and trips to the places involved.
- **Real stakes.** Someone's liberty, or a family's answers, depended on the outcome.

Every show below has at least three of the four. If you want the background on why Serial set the template, read [how Serial changed podcasting](/blog/how-serial-changed-podcasting).

## The best podcasts like Serial

### 1. In the Dark

**Best for: listeners who want the most rigorous version of Serial**

APM Reports' In the Dark, now published by The New Yorker, gives each season to one case and stays with it. **Season 2** is the one most often recommended to Serial fans. Madeleine Baran and her team spent a season on [Curtis Flowers](/cases/curtis-flowers), a Black man from Winona, Mississippi, who was tried six times for the same 1996 quadruple murder by the same district attorney. The team analysed that prosecutor's jury strikes and found Black prospective jurors were removed at nearly 4.4 times the rate of white ones.

In 2019 the US Supreme Court overturned Flowers' conviction on jury-selection grounds, the issue the show had spent a season documenting. The charges were dropped in 2020. Where Serial left you unsure, In the Dark builds a case and shows its working.

- **Seasons:** Season 1 covers the 1989 abduction of Jacob Wetterling and the failures of the investigation. Season 2 covers Curtis Flowers. Season 3 covers the 2005 killings of Iraqi civilians by US Marines in Haditha and won a Pulitzer Prize.
- **Style:** Measured and document-heavy, with a strong institutional focus.
- **Start with:** Season 2, Episode 1.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1148175292)

### 2. Your Own Backyard

**Best for: recreating the feeling of Serial Season 1**

Chris Lambert is a musician, not a journalist. He began looking into the 1996 disappearance of [Kristin Smart](/cases/kristin-smart), a Cal Poly student, because the case had gone nowhere for more than two decades. That outsider persistence is the closest thing on this list to Koenig in Season 1: you hear him working it out as he goes.

The difference is the ending. Lambert's reporting and the renewed attention around it preceded the 2021 arrest of Paul Flores, who was convicted of Smart's murder in October 2022. Her body has never been found.

- **Format:** A single case, told over one main season, with trial-era updates.
- **Style:** Personal, slow-building and local.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1480263708)

### 3. Bone Valley

**Best for: wrongful-conviction stories told like Serial**

Pulitzer-winning author Gilbert King spent the nine chapters of Bone Valley on Leo Schofield, convicted of the 1987 murder of his wife Michelle in Lakeland, Florida. The podcast argues that another man, Jeremy Scott, killed her. Like Serial, it is built on old case files, re-interviews and a narrator weighing up a convicted man's word.

Schofield was released on parole in April 2024. Polk County prosecutors still maintain he is guilty, so the questions the show raises are not settled.

- **Format:** A single case in nine chapters, plus bonus episodes following the parole process.
- **Style:** Cinematic and personal, with a clear point of view.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1643974551)

### 4. West Cork

**Best for: Serial's ambiguity, with no tidy answer**

In December 1996, the French film producer Sophie Toscan du Plantier was murdered outside her holiday home near Schull in West Cork, Ireland. Reporters Sam Bungey and Jennifer Forde spent years on the case and its chief suspect, the English journalist Ian Bailey. Bailey was never charged in Ireland. A French court convicted him in his absence in 2019, but Ireland refused to extradite him, and he died in 2024.

It is the show on this list that most resembles Serial Season 1's ending: a central figure you can't quite read, and a conclusion the reporters refuse to force. It began as an Audible Original and has been free on podcast apps since April 2021. See our [Irish true crime podcasts](/blog/best-irish-true-crime-podcasts) guide for more from the same region.

- **Format:** A single case in one finished series.
- **Style:** Literary, atmospheric and character-driven.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1556472772)

### 5. The Teacher's Pet

**Best for: reporting that changed a case's outcome**

Hedley Thomas of The Australian reinvestigated the 1982 disappearance of [Lynette Dawson](/cases/lynette-dawson) from Sydney's Northern Beaches. Her husband, former rugby league player Chris Dawson, had never been charged. The podcast was released in 2018. Dawson was arrested in December 2019, convicted of Lynette's murder in August 2022 and sentenced to 24 years in prison.

Structurally it's very close to Serial: one journalist, one case, and evidence laid out week by week. Note on availability: The Australian geoblocked the podcast in Australia in 2019 ahead of the trial. When we checked in November 2026, it was available on Apple Podcasts in the UK but not in the Australian store.

- **Format:** A single case, with later update episodes.
- **Style:** Dogged, newspaper-led and sometimes polemical.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1385379989) (not available in Australia) · [our Teacher's Pet profile](/podcasts/the-teachers-pet)

### 6. Breakdown

**Best for: a newspaper's direct answer to Serial**

The Atlanta Journal-Constitution launched Breakdown in 2015, a year after Serial and explicitly inspired by it, with reporter Bill Rankin narrating. **Season 1** spends seven episodes on Justin Chapman, convicted of setting the 2006 fire that killed his elderly neighbour in Bremen, Georgia. Like Koenig, Rankin doesn't claim to know whether Chapman did it. His argument is that the trial was not fair.

Later seasons each take a different Georgia case. Rankin retired from the show in 2025 after Season 11.

- **Format:** One case per season.
- **Style:** Plain-spoken courtroom reporting.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id992983540) · [our Breakdown profile](/podcasts/breakdown)

### 7. Bear Brook

**Best for: forensics and how cold cases get solved**

New Hampshire Public Radio's Jason Moon tells the story of four bodies found in barrels in Allenstown, New Hampshire, in 1985 and 2000. Over the decades, the case widens into an investigation of a killer who used multiple identities, Terry Rasmussen. It is also a clear explanation of how genetic genealogy cracked it. For more on that technique, read our explainer on [genetic genealogy in cold cases](/blog/genetic-genealogy-cold-cases).

It's less uncertain than Serial, because the main questions are answered, but the reporting is just as careful. Season 2 moves to Jason Carroll, serving life for a 1988 murder he says police coerced him into confessing to. That makes it a natural fit for Serial fans too.

- **Format:** One case per season.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1423306695) · [our Bear Brook profile](/podcasts/bear-brook)

### 8. Death in Ice Valley

**Best for: UK and European listeners who want an unsolved mystery**

In November 1970, a woman's badly burned body was found in the Isdalen valley near Bergen, Norway. She had several false identities and has never been named. The BBC World Service and Norway's NRK sent Marit Higraff and Neil McCarthy to reinvestigate the case in 2018. Listeners joined in through an online group, and the series later added episodes following leads they had found.

It has Serial's open-endedness without a courtroom. As far as we know, the Isdal Woman is still unidentified.

- **Format:** A single case.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1357695290) · [our Death in Ice Valley profile](/podcasts/death-in-ice-valley)

### 9. Missing & Murdered: Finding Cleo

**Best for: a family's search, reported with care**

CBC investigative reporter Connie Walker follows the family of Cleo Semaganis Nicotine. Cleo was a Cree girl taken from her Saskatchewan family by child welfare workers in the 1970s and adopted in the United States. Her family believed she had been killed while hitchhiking back home, and Walker follows the trail with her siblings.

It's quieter than Serial and more about a family than a verdict, but it shares the same commitment to finding out what actually happened.

- **Format:** A single case. It is the second season of CBC's Missing & Murdered series, after Who Killed Alberta Williams?
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1166556648) · [our Finding Cleo profile](/podcasts/missing-murdered-finding-cleo)

### 10. Someone Knows Something

**Best for: long, patient reinvestigations of cold cases**

CBC filmmaker David Ridgen gives each season to one unresolved Canadian case and works it alongside the victim's family. **Season 1** follows the 1972 disappearance of five-year-old Adrien McNaughton in Ontario. **Season 4** covers Wayne Greavette, killed in 1996 by a bomb disguised as a flashlight and mailed to his home near Puslinch, Ontario. Both are good starting points.

Ridgen is more of a presence than Koenig. He meets people face to face and sometimes confronts them. For more shows like this, see our [Canadian true crime podcasts](/blog/best-canadian-true-crime-podcasts) guide.

- **Format:** One case per season, with nine or more seasons so far.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1089216339) · [our Someone Knows Something profile](/podcasts/someone-knows-something)

### 11. Believed

**Best for: institutional failure rather than whodunnit**

Michigan Radio reporters Kate Wells and Lindsey Smith, with NPR, asked how Larry Nassar was able to abuse so many girls and young women for so long. They answer it through survivors' accounts, interviews with parents and detectives, and recordings from the police investigation. There's no mystery about who did it. The investigation is into who failed to listen.

It's a good pick if what stayed with you from Serial was the system, not the suspect. It is a single finished season from 2018.

- **Format:** A single case.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1438898081) · [our Believed profile](/podcasts/believed)

### 12. Cold

**Best for: deep local reporting on one disappearance**

KSL's Dave Cawley spent the first season on Susan Powell, who disappeared from West Valley City, Utah, in December 2009. Her husband Josh was the focus of suspicion but was never charged. In 2012 he killed the couple's two sons and himself. Cawley reconstructs the investigation and the years of family conflict that followed.

Later seasons each take a different cold case, including a new season on the Nancy Baird case that began in September 2026.

- **Format:** One case per season.
- **Listen:** [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1441595858) · [our Cold profile](/podcasts/cold)

## If you specifically loved Serial Season 1

If Season 1 is the one you can't stop thinking about, it helps to know what you liked about it:

- **The uncertainty about guilt:** go to **West Cork**, then **Breakdown** Season 1.
- **An outsider who won't let go:** go to **Your Own Backyard**.
- **The legal fight:** go to **In the Dark** Season 2, then **Bone Valley**.

You can also go deeper on the case itself. [Undisclosed](/podcasts/undisclosed) was started in 2015 by Rabia Chaudry, who first brought the case to Koenig, with attorneys Colin Miller and Susan Simpson. Its first season re-examines the evidence against Adnan Syed in far more legal detail than Serial attempted. For what happened after the podcast, including the 2022 vacatur, its 2023 reinstatement and the 2025 resentencing, read [Adnan Syed after Serial](/blog/adnan-syed-after-serial) or our [Hae Min Lee case file](/cases/adnan-syed-hae-min-lee).

## More from the team behind Serial

Serial Productions has made other documentary series that are not strictly true crime but have the same reported, first-person feel:

- **Serial Season 4: Guantánamo (2024).** Koenig and Dana Chivvis tell the history of the US prison camp through the people who worked and were held there.
- **[S-Town](/podcasts/s-town) (2017).** Brian Reed is drawn to Woodstock, Alabama, by an email about an alleged murder, and the story becomes something else entirely.
- **The Trojan Horse Affair (2022).** Brian Reed and Hamza Syed investigate who wrote the anonymous letter behind the 2014 "Trojan Horse" schools scandal in Birmingham.
