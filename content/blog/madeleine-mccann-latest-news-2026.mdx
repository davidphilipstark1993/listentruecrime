Nearly nineteen years and four months after Madeleine McCann disappeared from Praia da Luz, the investigation remains active across the United Kingdom, Germany and Portugal. No one has been charged in connection with her disappearance, and her body has never been found. This is a snapshot of where things stood as of August 2026 — for the full background, see our [complete timeline](/blog/madeleine-mccann-timeline) and our [explainer on suspect Christian Brueckner](/blog/who-is-christian-brueckner-madeleine-mccann).

## Operation Grange Remains Active

[Operation Grange](/blog/operation-grange-explained), the Metropolitan Police's dedicated investigation, is still open, with cumulative documented costs exceeding £13 million since it began in 2011. The force has said it continues to work with German and Portuguese counterparts and will pursue any viable leads. Reporting in 2026 has suggested renewed focus on Brueckner specifically, with some coverage framing the aim as building a case strong enough for the Crown Prosecution Service to authorise charges before the 20th anniversary of Madeleine's disappearance in May 2027 — though that timeline hasn't been officially confirmed by the Met.

## The June 2025 Search Near Atalaia

In early June 2025, German and Portuguese officers conducted a multi-day search of scrubland, wells and abandoned structures near Atalaia, close to the Ocean Club resort, in an area linked to Brueckner's known movements. Some reporting described the operation as significant, though authorities released no public detail of any findings that could be used as evidence — a pattern consistent with every search connected to this case since 2007.

## A German Dossier Reaches the Met

British media reported during 2026 that German investigators had handed Scotland Yard a substantial dossier of material relating to Brueckner, described by some sources as containing photographs and witness accounts. Some coverage used dramatic language — "explosive," "sensational" — to describe the contents; none of that framing has been confirmed by the Metropolitan Police, which has said only that it remains in close discussion with German and Portuguese colleagues. Readers should treat characterisations like that as media framing rather than confirmed fact until an official source says otherwise.

<Callout type="warning">
Being handed a dossier of material is not the same as having enough evidence to charge someone. As of August 2026, the Crown Prosecution Service has not authorised charges against Brueckner, and no formal UK arrest warrant or extradition request has been publicly confirmed.
</Callout>

## Could a UK Trial Actually Happen?

The most significant development of 2026 has been the Metropolitan Police's exploration of whether Brueckner could be charged in Britain and, if so, whether he could be extradited to stand trial. UK law does allow, in certain circumstances, for the murder of a British subject to be prosecuted domestically even when the alleged offence happened overseas.

The legal obstacles are real, though. Germany's Basic Law generally prohibits extraditing its own citizens to countries outside the European Union, and the post-Brexit UK–EU Trade and Cooperation Agreement doesn't override that constitutional protection. Legal commentary has generally described extradition as a distant prospect — the more realistic route, if sufficient evidence exists, would be a prosecution in Germany or Portugal rather than the UK.

## Brueckner's Situation Since Release

Brueckner, now in his late forties, was released from Sehnde prison in Germany on 17 September 2025 after completing his seven-year sentence for the unrelated 2005 rape of an elderly American woman in Praia da Luz. He has denied any involvement in Madeleine's disappearance throughout. Since release, he's lived under police monitoring conditions in northern Germany. In 2026 he faced additional, separate police attention in Germany, reportedly including scrutiny over an encounter with officers — a matter unrelated to the McCann investigation itself.

German prosecutors maintain he is their only suspect in the McCann case, but have said there is currently no prospect of an indictment, citing insufficient evidence to meet the required legal threshold. Separately, German federal prosecutors have continued pushing to overturn Brueckner's October 2024 acquittal on unrelated rape and child sex offence charges and secure a retrial — a process that could affect what evidence becomes available to both the German and British cases.

## What Comes Next

Several threads remain open heading toward the case's 20th anniversary in May 2027: continued analysis of the German dossier by Operation Grange detectives, the possibility of further searches or forensic work in Portugal, and Germany's own effort to strengthen a domestic prosecution. Kate and Gerry McCann continue to support the Find Madeleine fund and have said, publicly, that they haven't given up hope of answers.

Madeleine McCann has now been missing for more than 7,000 days. Authorities in three countries continue to appeal for information about the night of 3 May 2007.
