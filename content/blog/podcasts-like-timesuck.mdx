The best podcasts like Timesuck are **Last Podcast on the Left**, **Behind the Bastards**, **The Dollop** and **Small Town Murder**: comedian-led shows that research a dark subject properly and then joke their way through it. If it's Dan Cummins himself you like, his paranormal show **Scared To Death** is the obvious next step.

Timesuck works because it doesn't stick to one lane. In a single month Dan Cummins might cover a serial killer, a cult and a strange episode of history. Recent episodes include the killer cop Gerard Schaefer, the Cult of the Church with No Name and the Candy Mossler murder case. Main episodes in recent weeks have run between about two and two and a half hours, with shorter "Short Suck" episodes of around an hour in between. The official description promises "dark humor with deep research", and that's a fair summary.

So "a show like Timesuck" can mean several different things. This list is split by the part of Timesuck you'd most like more of. Every show was checked against its official podcast listing and feed in November 2026.

## The closest overall match

### Last Podcast on the Left

If you could only try one show after Timesuck, make it this one. Last Podcast on the Left describes itself as covering "all the horrors our world has to offer, both imagined and real", and that range is the reason it fits: serial killers and cults, but also strange history and the paranormal. Two recent episodes were about the gorillas of Virunga and the story of Harambe, which shows how wide it ranges.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** big subjects researched in depth, multi-part series on the heavier topics, and comedians who aren't afraid to be crude.

**How it differs:** Timesuck is one storyteller. Last Podcast is a three-way conversation. Marcus Parks does the research and narrates, while Henry Zebrowski and Ed Larson riff on it. The jokes come faster and go further, and if you like Dan's tighter delivery the crosstalk can take an episode or two to get used to.

## For the weird history

### Behind the Bastards

Robert Evans picks one of history's worst people and explains, usually over two or more episodes, how they got that way. Each topic has a comedian, writer or podcaster as a guest who hears the story for the first time. The current run includes a two-parter on Paul Mackenzie, the Kenyan pastor behind a doomsday starvation cult.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** this is the "notorious historical figures" side of Timesuck on its own. The research goes deep and the guest's reactions keep it funny.

**How it differs:** it's about bad people rather than crimes as such, and Evans is open about his politics. The same feed also carries the network's weekly *It Could Happen Here* episodes, which are current affairs rather than history, so pick from the "Part One" episodes if you're new.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/behind-the-bastards/id1373812661) or search "Behind the Bastards" in any podcast app.

### The Dollop

Dave Anthony tells Gareth Reynolds a story from history, and Gareth doesn't know what it is until it starts. That simple set-up makes the show work: Gareth's disbelief stands in for yours, and both are quick to act out the scenes. Most stories are from American history, and the bigger ones run over several parts. The recent series on the Jeannette Expedition, a doomed 19th-century Arctic voyage, ran to four episodes.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** the same pleasure of a comedian walking you through an episode of history you'd never heard of, where the truth turns out to be stranger than you expected.

**How it differs:** it's history first, with crime only now and then, and it's improvised and silly where Timesuck is more of a structured lecture. Episodes are shorter, at around an hour.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/the-dollop-with-dave-anthony-and-gareth-reynolds/id643055307) or search "The Dollop" in any podcast app.

## For the comedian-led true crime

### Small Town Murder

The show's own description: "Two comedians look at a small town, what makes it tick, and a murder that took place there." James Pietragallo and Jimmie Whisman spend the first part of each episode on the town itself (its history, its local news, its oddities) before they reach the crime. Main episodes are long, around three hours recently, and there's a shorter episode most weekends.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** very long episodes, genuine research and plenty of jokes. If you put Timesuck on for a long drive, this works the same way.

**How it differs:** it's American murder cases only, with no history or paranormal side. The humour is closer to the bone than Dan's, and it jokes about the killers and the circumstances in ways some listeners won't like. Our [comedy true crime guide](/blog/best-comedy-true-crime-podcasts) covers that question in more detail.

### Crime in Sports

The same two hosts, aimed at athletes who've been in trouble with the law. Recent episodes include a multi-part series on the hockey enforcer Bill "Goldie" Goldthorpe, and episodes run at around two hours.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** it has the Small Town Murder format and humour, and the stories are often more ridiculous than tragic.

**How it differs:** it's narrow. If you don't care about sport at all, some background sections will drag, although the hosts don't assume you're a fan.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/crime-in-sports/id1082678396) or search "Crime in Sports" in any podcast app.

## For more Dan Cummins

### Scared To Death

Dan Cummins' other podcast, hosted with his wife Lynze Cummins. In the official description, Dan's goal is "to scare Lynze with creepy accounts of demonic possession, shadow people, and other eerie phenomena". Then Lynze reads out stories sent in by listeners. Episodes run around an hour.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** it's the same host and the same delivery, and Lynze's reactions add something Timesuck doesn't have.

**How it differs:** there's no history and no true crime. It's ghosts, possessions and the unexplained. If the paranormal episodes are your favourite Timesucks, start here. If you skip them, skip this.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/scared-to-death/id1477287015) or search "Scared To Death" in any podcast app.

## For true crime with lighter laughs

### Morbid

Morbid calls itself "a true crime, creepy history and all things spooky podcast hosted by an autopsy technician and a hairstylist": Alaina Urquhart and Ash Kelley. That mix of crime, history and spooky stories is close to Timesuck's. Recent episodes range from the Barefoot Bandit and Bernhard Goetz to a haunted Wisconsin house. New episodes come out twice a week.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** the same blend of topics, real research, and Alaina's forensic background, which gives the medical details more weight than most shows manage.

**How it differs:** it's warmer and more chatty than Timesuck, with less edge and shorter episodes. It's a good choice if Timesuck sometimes feels like too much.

### Sinisterhood

Its own description is "the comedy podcast about all things sinister, covering true crime, cults, cryptids, unsolved mysteries, and strange phenomena". The hosts are Dallas comedians Christie Wallace and Heather McKinney, and Heather is a licensed attorney, so the legal side gets more attention than usual. Main episodes come out on Wednesdays, with listener stories on Fridays.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** of all the shows here, its range of topics is the closest to Timesuck's, and the two hosts really are comedians.

**How it differs:** it's a conversation between two friends rather than one narrator, and the stories are often split across parts. The current example is a two-parter on the Haunted Hahn Mansion.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/sinisterhood/id1391483100) or search "Sinisterhood" in any podcast app.

### And That's Why We Drink

Christine Schiefer and Em Schulz split every episode in two: one paranormal story and one true crime story. New episodes come out on Sundays and run for about two hours.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** it's long, and the mix of crime and the paranormal is like getting a Timesuck and a Scared To Death in one sitting.

**How it differs:** it's the lightest on research of these shows and the most about the hosts' friendship. Expect lots of tangents. It's a comfort listen more than a deep dive.

## For the long, curious deep dive

### You're Wrong About

Sarah Marshall, a journalist, re-examines a person or event the public got wrong. That includes moral panics, scandals, and crimes that were reported one way and turned out to be something else. Most episodes have a guest, and recent ones have run between one and two hours.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** Timesuck often sets out to correct the version of a story you thought you knew, and this whole show does that. It's funny in a dry, conversational way.

**How it differs:** it's about empathy, not shock. Marshall is more interested in why everyone got a story wrong than in the grisly details.

### Lore

Aaron Mahnke narrates "dark historical tales": folklore, strange deaths, unusual places and the true stories behind legends. Main episodes are around 25 to 30 minutes.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** it covers the same strange, dark corners of history that Timesuck goes into, in short episodes you can finish on a commute.

**How it differs:** there are no jokes. Lore is quiet and atmospheric, closer to a ghost story told by the fire. It suits days when you want the subject matter without the comedy.

### Dan Carlin's Hardcore History

For listeners who stay with Timesuck for the length, not the laughs. Dan Carlin tells huge stretches of history (wars, empires, atrocities) as narrated epics. Recent shows in his "Mania for Subjugation" series run for about four hours each.

**Why Timesuck fans like it:** one host telling one story at great length, and he's very good at it. Carlin is open that he isn't a historian, and the show is about the drama and the human experience rather than an academic account.

**How it differs:** it's serious and there's no comedy, and new shows come out months apart. Only the most recent shows are in the free feed. Older series are sold separately on Carlin's website.

Listen on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/dan-carlins-hardcore-history/id173001861) or search "Hardcore History" in any podcast app.
