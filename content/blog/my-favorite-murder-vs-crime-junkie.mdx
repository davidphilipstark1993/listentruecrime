Crime Junkie and My Favorite Murder are arguably the two biggest names in mainstream true crime podcasting, and they get compared constantly — usually badly, as though the only difference is which host you find funnier. That's not really it. These two shows are built around different jobs entirely, and the honest answer to "which one" depends on which job you actually want done.

## The comedy question

My Favorite Murder invented the modern comedy-true-crime format, and it's still the more purely comedic of the two. Karen Kilgariff and Georgia Hardstark don't pretend to be authorities — they're friends riffing on cases, with tangents, self-deprecation, and a fair amount of "let me look this up while we're talking" transparency about their own research process. It's rated 5/10 on binge factor here, not because it's weak but because a lot of any given episode isn't really about the case at all.

Crime Junkie has almost none of that. Ashley Flowers and Brit Prawat deliver a case with minimal personal digression — the show's entire appeal rests on getting you the story efficiently, every week, without filler. If you find MFM's tangents charming, Crime Junkie will feel clinical by comparison. If you find them frustrating, Crime Junkie will feel like a relief.

**Verdict:** if the comedy and the friendship are the actual product you're buying, My Favorite Murder wins outright — Crime Junkie isn't trying to compete on this axis at all.

## The reliability question

Here Crime Junkie has the clear edge. A new case every Monday, 30-45 minutes, consistent structure — Flowers has kept that promise across hundreds of episodes, and it's rated **Must listen** here for exactly that reliability. My Favorite Murder's episodes run 60-120 minutes and the ratio of case content to conversation varies a lot from week to week; some episodes are dense with research, others are mostly the hosts talking about their week. Neither approach is wrong, but if you want a predictable commute-length listen you can set a clock by, Crime Junkie is simply the more disciplined show.

**Verdict:** Crime Junkie, clearly, if consistency and pacing matter to you more than personality.

## The community question

My Favorite Murder didn't just build an audience — it built an identity. "Murderino" is a real, self-organizing fan community with its own merchandise, live tour, and shared vocabulary, and that community is arguably as much a part of the product as the episodes themselves. Crime Junkie has a large and genuinely devoted following, but it hasn't generated anything comparable in scale or self-awareness. If part of what you want from a podcast is the feeling of joining something, MFM offers that in a way Crime Junkie, structurally, isn't set up to.

**Verdict:** My Favorite Murder, and it isn't close.

## So which one?

Neither show is trying to be the other, which is why the "vs" framing slightly undersells both of them. Start with **Crime Junkie** if you want a reliable weekly habit and don't yet know if true crime podcasts are for you. Start with **My Favorite Murder** if the idea of a podcast as a standing appointment with two funny friends — case included — sounds more appealing than a news briefing. Plenty of listeners end up with both, for exactly the reasons above: they're not actually solving the same problem.
