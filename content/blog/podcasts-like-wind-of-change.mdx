If you loved Wind of Change, start with The Missing Cryptoqueen, then Catch and Kill, then Agent of Betrayal. All three are about someone who was hiding something, and the first two follow a reporter across borders and through a lot of dead ends. The other six suit different moods, from 1970s Washington to a small Alabama town.

## What made Wind of Change work

[Wind of Change](/podcasts/wind-of-change) is Patrick Radden Keefe, the New Yorker writer behind Say Nothing and Empire of Pain, chasing a rumour from a trusted source: that the CIA, not the Scorpions, wrote the 1990s power ballad. It came from Pineapple Street Studios, Crooked Media and Spotify, and the feed released eight episodes weekly from 11 May to 29 June 2020.

The trail runs from Kyiv and Moscow to a GI Joe convention in Ohio, through the 1989 Moscow Music Peace Festival and the band's manager Doc McGhee, who, the episode description says, "played a role in one of the largest drug busts in U.S. history". In the last episode Keefe meets Klaus Meine in Hanover, and Meine denies it.

The show never proves the theory, and it doesn't pretend to. Slate's reviewer put it well: "It's not much of a spoiler to say that the podcast doesn't prove that anyone else did." That is the charm. It's true crime adjacent, more espionage and propaganda than a crime case, so the picks below lean further into real crimes: fraud, espionage, corruption and a manhunt.

## A reporter chasing someone who vanished

### The Missing Cryptoqueen

If you only try one, make it [The Missing Cryptoqueen](/podcasts/the-missing-cryptoqueen). Jamie Bartlett, for BBC Sounds, goes after Dr Ruja Ignatova, who led OneCoin, a scheme the FBI says affected millions of investors, until October 2017, and then disappeared. The BBC's own line is that she "persuaded millions to join her financial revolution. Then she disappeared."

She is charged in the US with wire fraud, securities fraud and money laundering offences, and she has not been convicted. The FBI added her to its Ten Most Wanted list in 2022, and a US State Department programme is offering up to $5 million for information leading to her arrest or conviction. She has not been found.

The first eight episodes came out in 2019, and the BBC has kept adding to it, most recently with four new episodes on 22 January 2026. Most run around 25 to 45 minutes, with a couple close to an hour. Start with "1. Dr Ruja". The caveat: it's a long story with real victims who lost money, and there's no ending yet.

### Chameleon: Hollywood Con Queen

[Chameleon: Hollywood Con Queen](/podcasts/chameleon-hollywood-con-queen) is from Campside Media and Sony Music Entertainment, with the journalists Josh Dean and Vanessa Grigoriadis. Someone was impersonating powerful women in the film business and luring people to Indonesia for film jobs that didn't exist. The hosts set out to find out who.

Hargobind Tahilramani is accused of being behind it. He is charged in California with wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft, and has not been convicted. In July 2025 he lost a High Court challenge to his extradition from the UK to the US. I couldn't find a reliable source on what has happened since.

The caveat is access. The series first came out in 2020, and the free feed now has only the trailer and "Chapter 1: The Hook", around 48 minutes. The rest sits behind The Binge, a paid subscription.

## Spies, minders and people who aren't who they say

### Catch and Kill

[Catch and Kill](/podcasts/catch-and-kill) is Ronan Farrow's companion podcast to his reporting on Harvey Weinstein, from Pineapple Street Studios. Episode 1, "The Spy", is the Wind of Change hook: Igor Ostrovskiy, a private investigator in New York, was contracted in 2017 to watch Farrow for a secret client. That client turned out to be Black Cube, an Israel-based agency working for Weinstein.

There are nine episodes from November 2019 to February 2020, plus a conversation with Rose McGowan, mostly around 35 to 50 minutes. For the legal position: in September 2026 Weinstein was sentenced to 15 years in New York for a first-degree criminal sexual act, after a 2025 retrial, and he continues to deny the allegations. The caveat: this is a story about sexual assault allegations, and the women in it are real people.

### Agent of Betrayal: The Double Life of Robert Hanssen

[Agent of Betrayal](/podcasts/agent-of-betrayal-the-double-life-of-robert-hanssen) is CBS News, with Major Garrett. Robert Hanssen was an FBI agent who sold secrets to the Soviet Union and later Russia for two decades. The FBI says he pleaded guilty to 15 counts of espionage in 2001 and was sentenced to life without parole in 2002. He died in June 2023, aged 79.

It's eight weekly episodes from autumn 2023, around 35 to 50 minutes each. Start with "The Spy Next Door". The episode I'd point a Wind of Change fan to is "Wrong Man", about the CIA officer the FBI suspected before it found Hanssen. The caveat: Garrett knows the ending from the start, so it's a portrait more than a chase.

## Cold War secrets and political crime, told with a wink

### Bag Man

[Bag Man](/podcasts/bag-man) is Rachel Maddow's first original podcast, from MSNBC, now MS NOW in the feed. It's about Spiro Agnew, Richard Nixon's vice president, who was accused of taking bribes. In October 1973 he resigned and pleaded no contest to one count of federal income tax evasion. The bribery case is what prosecutors said, not something he was convicted of.

Seven episodes came out between October and December 2018, around 25 to 45 minutes, with a bonus in 2023. Start with "Episode 1: An Unsettling Secret". At about four hours in all, it's one of the shortest binges here. The caveat: it's American political history, and it helps to know a little about Watergate.

### Slow Burn, season 1

[Slow Burn](/podcasts/slow-burn) is Slate's history series, and season 1 is Watergate with Leon Neyfakh, from late 2017. It asks what it felt like to live through the scandal while nobody knew how it would end, which is the Wind of Change mood exactly. Start with "1. Martha", about Martha Mitchell, who tried to blow the whistle and was called crazy for it.

Two caveats. The shared feed is now mostly Slate's Decoder Ring between seasons, and the newest season, Becoming Justice Gorsuch, is about something else entirely, so pick season 1 by name. And when I checked on 27 November 2026, the free feed had full versions of only the first two episodes; from episode 3 on, they were short previews or marked for Slate Plus members.

## A reporter who goes in deep and gets more than they bargained for

### Hunting Warhead

[Hunting Warhead](/podcasts/hunting-warhead) is from CBC Podcasts and the Norwegian newspaper VG, hosted by Daemon Fairless. It follows VG's journalist Håkon Høydal and the hacker Einar Stangvik, and the police, in the hunt for the man behind the username "Warhead", who ran a dark web site sharing child abuse material with more than a million users.

Season 1 is six episodes from November 2019, around 45 to 65 minutes. Start with "Episode 1: Hacker vs. Hacker". The caveat is the heaviest on this list: it's about the abuse of children. It isn't graphic, but it is hard to hear. The same feed now carries a second season from 2026, Hunting the Suicide Salesman, about the online sale of products linked to suicides.

### S-Town

[S-Town](/podcasts/s-town) is Brian Reed, from Serial Productions. A man called John B. McLemore asked Reed to come to his Alabama town and look into a wealthy family's son who was said to be bragging that he'd got away with murder. It turns into something else entirely, which is the feeling Wind of Change gives you in its middle episodes.

All seven chapters came out at once on 28 March 2017, around 45 to 60 minutes each. Start with Chapter I. The caveat: McLemore died by suicide in 2015, before the series came out, and it reveals a lot about his private life. His estate sued the producers, and the case was settled in 2020. Please take care with this one.

## A rumour that won't die: The Improvement Association

[The Improvement Association](/podcasts/the-improvement-association) is Zoe Chace for Serial Productions and The New York Times, from 2021. In Bladen County, North Carolina, some people accuse a Black advocacy group of cheating in elections. The show's own description says those accusations "have never been substantiated, but they persist." Chace goes to find out why.

That's the Wind of Change question turned on a small town: why do people believe a story nobody can prove? It's five chapters, around 40 to 50 minutes. The caveat is the paywall: the free feed shows only the trailer and "Chapter One: 'The Big Shadoo'", and full access needs a New York Times subscription.

## How to choose

When I checked the feeds on 27 November 2026, this is how they sorted:

- **Shortest binge:** The Improvement Association, five chapters and just under four hours (only Chapter One is free), or Bag Man, about four hours and all free.
- **Still adding episodes:** The Missing Cryptoqueen, which added four in January 2026, and Hunting Warhead, whose feed has a 2026 second season.
- **A spy story proper:** Agent of Betrayal, then Catch and Kill.
- **A con artist:** The Missing Cryptoqueen or Chameleon: Hollywood Con Queen.
- **Outside the US:** The Missing Cryptoqueen from the BBC, and Hunting Warhead from CBC with Norway's VG.
- **If you liked Keefe himself:** the Wind of Change feed carries previews of his books Rogues and London Falling.

## An honest note

Wind of Change is fun precisely because it never lands the punch. Some of the shows below do have a court result, and several have real victims still living with what happened, from OneCoin investors to the children in Hunting Warhead. I've written more about that in [the ethics of true crime podcasting](/blog/ethics-of-true-crime-podcasting).
